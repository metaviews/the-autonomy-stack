# Pattern: Procedural Entombment

## Summary
Process crystallizes into permanent form, immune to revision, converting the mechanisms of accountability into the substance of permanence.

## Core Dynamic
Each procedure was created for defensible reasons in a specific context. Over time, procedures accumulate, interlock, and become load-bearing — other procedures depend on them; institutional roles are organized around them; the people who know how to navigate them gain structural power by doing so. Eventually, changing any given procedure requires following the procedure being changed. The system becomes self-sealing: the formal path to reform requires engaging the architecture that generates the problem. No individual rule is indefensible; the accumulated weight is not visible as a governance failure until an attempt at reform makes it visible.

## Signals
- Changing a rule requires demonstrating compliance with the rule being changed
- Historic procedures are required as precedent even when the conditions that produced them no longer exist
- Rules about how rules can be changed are themselves treated as unchangeable through formal process
- Institutional knowledge concentrates in those who navigate procedure, who then have structural interest in its maintenance
- Appeals to the purpose or spirit of a rule are ruled out of order by the letter of the rule
- Efforts to reform the procedure are absorbed by the procedure: a review committee is formed; the committee's recommendations require a further process; the process stalls
- The procedure for changing procedure has itself never been successfully invoked

## What It Targets
The revisability of governance — the capacity to adapt, learn, and correct. Principle 8 (Nothing Is Final) violated not by declaration but by accumulated structure. When governance cannot update itself, legitimacy eventually requires bypassing it, which the procedure treats as illegitimate.

## Why It Works
Every individual procedure is defensible in isolation. The failure mode is systemic, not local, and it emerges slowly enough that no single moment of crystallization is visible. The only apparent alternative to procedure is to bypass it entirely — which is punished as a norm violation. The entombed system thus generates its own pressure for informal workarounds, which it then uses to delegitimize those who have been forced outside formal process.

## Common Misreadings
- "There are rules for a reason"
- "The process is there to protect everyone equally"
- "Work to change it through proper channels"
- "Breaking procedure sets a dangerous precedent"
- "If the process is slow, that's a feature — it prevents hasty decisions"
