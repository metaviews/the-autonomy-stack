# Pattern: Commons Destruction

## Summary
Shared resources, institutions, and relational infrastructure are systematically enclosed, privatized, or degraded to concentrate agency.

## Core Dynamic
Control of essential material and informational bases shifts from collective stewardship to private or state monopolies, converting public access into conditional privilege.

## Signals
- National security or crisis narratives justify bypassing public accountability for infrastructure
- Critical systems rely on single-vendor lock-ins that bypass state oversight
- Legal authority becomes ceremonial when physical or digital substrates are privatized
- Data consolidation is masked by public ethical pledges
- Shared factual baselines fragment as institutions prioritize factional survival

## What It Targets
Independence, collective capacity, and the shared material conditions required for autonomous action.

## Why It Works
It exploits the material necessity of shared infrastructure to enforce dependency, making resistance expensive and exclusionary.

## Common Misreadings
- "This is just necessary for security"
- "Private efficiency is better than public management"
- "Partnerships are the future of governance"
- "Consolidation is a natural result of market competition"
