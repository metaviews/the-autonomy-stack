# Pattern: Algorithmic Authority

## Summary
Automated systems accumulate decision-making power by mimicking institutional authority without inheriting its accountability structures.

## Core Dynamic
Binding decisions delegate to opaque systems that resist review while claiming operational neutrality.

## Signals
- Algorithms making binding decisions on employment, lending, or movement without human appeal
- "Black box" logic cited as proprietary security to block contestation
- Engagement metrics replacing editorial or normative gatekeeping in shaping public outcomes
- Language mutation ("algo-speak") by users to evade automated moderation
- State or corporate framing of automated scaling as a strategic necessity over democratic oversight

## What It Targets
Contestability of decisions, human oversight, and the capacity to challenge binding rulings.

## Why It Works
It exploits the demand for scale and efficiency, framing opacity as a functional necessity.

## Common Misreadings
- "This is just neutral optimization"
- "Algorithms can't be biased"
- "Humans are still in the loop"
