# Pattern: Semantic Capture

## Summary
Governance vocabulary is colonized by technical or market language, narrowing what can be thought and contested before any formal decision is made.

## Core Dynamic
Terms that once named social relationships, collective obligations, or political commitments are systematically replaced by terms that name market transactions or technical processes. The replacement arrives through normal usage in authoritative contexts — corporate communications, policy documents, platform interfaces — until the original meaning becomes archaic and the new meaning becomes the only available register. Once the vocabulary has shifted, argument within it reproduces the assumptions of those who set the terms: contesting outcomes while accepting the language that produces them. The colonization is not declared; it is accomplished through repetition until no declaration is necessary.

## Signals
- "Freedom" in policy documents means consumer choice; "community" in corporate communications means user base; "security" means data protection or physical control
- Governance discussions adopt market metrics — efficiency, growth, return on investment — without naming the substitution
- Critics of technical systems are told to understand "how it works" before objecting, making technical fluency a prerequisite for political speech
- Terms for collective action (solidarity, mutual aid, commons) are treated as retrograde, utopian, or unscalable
- Debate about goals is replaced by debate about optimization: the question is not what we are doing but how to do it more efficiently
- Introducing pre-market vocabulary into technical or policy discussions is met with confusion or derision
- Dissent is named in the colonizer's language before it can name itself in its own

## What It Targets
The terrain on which contestation occurs. By capturing the vocabulary, semantic colonization forecloses arguments that depend on the displaced terms — not through censorship but through making those arguments unintelligible within the accepted register.

## Why It Works
Language replacement is gradual and diffuse; no single actor is responsible; the new vocabulary arrives pre-loaded with the assumptions of the powerful and presents itself as neutral description. Resisting it requires arguing in a register that sounds archaic to participants who have already adopted the new vocabulary — a disadvantage that compounds with each cycle of displacement.

## Common Misreadings
- "That's just language evolving naturally"
- "Everyone uses those terms now — you can't turn back the clock"
- "You're arguing about semantics when there are real problems to address"
- "The new language is more precise"
- "Holding onto old terminology is nostalgic"
