# ROADMAP: The Autonomy Stack

*A living document. Last updated: 2026-03-29.*

---

## What This Is

A roadmap for moving the Autonomy Stack from reference architecture toward operational framework.

It is not a build plan. It does not have fixed timelines, owners, or feature releases. It is structured around **maturation stages** — each stage deepening the Stack's grounding, extending its reach, or operationalizing its architecture.

The roadmap reflects the Stack's own principles. It treats uncertainty about sequencing as a design condition, not a failure. It names genuine disagreements rather than resolving them prematurely. It builds in review moments — points where the project should stop, assess what changed, and revise accordingly.

A new collaborator reading this should be able to understand where the project is going and why. The project should be able to return to this document as conditions change.

---

## Where We Are

The Stack is at the end of a first major phase: **reference architecture**.

v0.3 establishes the foundational frame — layers as dimensions of agency, each describing conditions under which autonomy is possible, sustainable, and contestable. Eighteen months of applied research corpus (the *Metaviews: Future of Authority* newsletter) has been extracted, audited, and integrated. Fifteen patterns are live. Five module candidates are documented with recommended sequencing. Eight unresolved provocations have been named.

The corpus itself suggests the trajectory: three stages — Crisis Detection (Oct–Dec 2024), Institutional Failure (Jan–Jun 2025), Alternative Construction (Jul 2025–Mar 2026). The Stack is entering its own third stage. The analytical work is in place. The question is no longer *what is happening* but *what should be built*.

That shift defines this roadmap.

---

## The Condition Going In

Several things are true at the start of this phase that the roadmap must be honest about.

**The architecture is more stable than the vocabulary.** The six layers are established. But 330+ concepts from the corpus remain unorganized. Without a shared lexicon, the framework is inaccessible to practitioners and difficult for automated systems to work with consistently.

**The pattern library is uneven.** Tier 1 patterns (Algorithmic Authority, Epistemic Warfare, Legitimacy Drift) are well-grounded. Several Tier 2 and Tier 3 patterns need deepening or stress-testing. And several patterns named in existing documents — Proceduralism as Shield, Procedural Entombment, Collective Leadership Architecture — remain unwritten.

**The eight provocations are real.** They are not problems to route around. Some are addressable in the near term; some require sustained framework development; some may be permanent design conditions — tensions the Stack must learn to name and navigate rather than resolve. This roadmap is explicit about which is which.

**Agentic computing is no longer deferred.** The Stack was designed for legibility by both humans and machines. In v0.3, agentic operationalization was treated as future work. That future has arrived. Governance questions — how authority is delegated to non-human agents, how contestability functions at machine speed, how legibility is maintained when reasoning is automated — now belong inside the framework, not deferred to a later version.

**The audience is growing.** The Stack is ready to be usable by practitioners, institutions, and automated systems. That requires explicit interface work: design toolkits, case studies, applied frameworks, and entry points for non-specialist readers.

## Stage 1: Grounding

*The deepest work should happen first.*

### Module: Food Power & Agricultural Sovereignty

The most developed module candidate. Design thinking spans all six layers. Concrete proposals already exist in the corpus — the 10-Point Food Power Program, community resource pooling frameworks, dual-mandate governance models separating domestic stability from export innovation.

**Work to do:**
- Write the module document: core framework, design principles, governance architecture
- Develop the dual-track model (domestic security / export innovation) as a reusable design pattern
- Articulate "agricultural data as public infrastructure" as a legibility design requirement
- Name one concrete institutional experiment: a regional food security system with explicit governance commitments to open data, labor standards, and dual-mandate authority

**Provocation this module must sit with:** P7 (material base as authority mechanism). The module cannot treat food systems as technical infrastructure. Food control is a power mechanism — a direct lever of coercion and dependency. The module needs explicit language for who controls what, on what terms, and what contestability looks like from the position of those most dependent.

**Gaps to acknowledge:** Global trade agreement integration, climate volatility response, and Indigenous land sovereignty are not solved by a module document. They should be named as open conditions, not omitted.

---

### Module: Care-Based Infrastructure Design

The most conceptually advanced in its treatment of care as governance principle rather than support condition. The shift from "taxpayers to caretakers" as a legitimacy frame is ready to be operationalized.

**Work to do:**
- Write the module document: define care infrastructure, articulate "shared endurance" as operational principle, distinguish care-as-support from care-as-authority
- Develop "failure management as design" as a concrete framework: systems that are designed to acknowledge constraint and loss, not to project endless responsiveness
- Write authority structure guidance: authority measured by responsibility under constraint, not by responsiveness on demand
- Build care-based metrics: what gets measured, by whom, with what contestability built in

**Provocations this module must sit with:** P2 (care-based authority resisting contestation) and P8 (care capacity inversion).

The module cannot romanticize relational governance. Care creates legitimacy through relational efficacy — but that legitimacy also resists formal contestation. The module must develop the distinction between care-as-support (resource provision) and care-as-authority (decision-making power), and articulate how the latter stays contestable without undermining the former.

The deeper problem: the module requires care capacity that existing systems have been designed to eliminate. It should name this explicitly. Designing care-based infrastructure in conditions of care scarcity is not the same as designing it in conditions of care abundance. The module should say what it assumes and what it requires.

---

### Pattern Maturation: Tier 1 Deepening

**Epistemic Warfare** and **Legitimacy Drift** are the analytically most important Tier 1 patterns. Both have accumulated new supporting material from the corpus since they were written. Work for Stage 1:

- Stress-test each pattern: under what conditions does the pattern fail to describe what it claims to describe? What would falsify it?
- Add new signals from the 2025–2026 corpus
- Cross-pattern analysis: how do these two patterns interact? What does Legitimacy Drift look like when Epistemic Warfare is its proximate cause? Write this as an explicit section in one or both patterns.

---

### New Patterns: Three Unwritten

These patterns are named in existing Stack documents but not yet written. Each should follow the 7-section structure (Summary / Core Dynamic / Signals / What It Targets / Why It Works / Common Misreadings / Related Patterns).

**Proceduralism as Shield** — Using procedural compliance to forestall substantive contestation. Distinct from Institutional Weaponization in that procedure is deployed not to attack an opponent but to insulate decision-makers from accountability while appearing legitimate. Engages Provocations P1 and P4.

**Procedural Entombment** — The crystallization of process into permanent form, immune to revision. What begins as a reasonable procedure becomes, over time, the mechanism by which contestation itself is absorbed and neutralized. The opposite of Principle 8 (Nothing Is Final) operationalized as a governance failure mode.

**Collective Leadership Architecture** — The governance design of distributed leadership without central command. Less a failure mode than the others — closer to a design pattern for distributed resistance. Engages Module 5 and Provocation P4 directly: how does collective leadership maintain coherence when conditions require rapid response?

---

### Lexicon: Foundation Layer

The corpus produced 330+ concept entries. A Stack lexicon would make the framework self-contained and accessible without requiring readers to trace back through 295 extraction files.

**Stage 1 scope:** 50–75 core terms. Selection criteria:
- Appears in 5+ extraction files (see AUDIT.md recurring concepts)
- Named but not defined in current Stack documents
- Used differently across contexts, requiring explicit disambiguation

**Format:** Consistent entry structure — term, brief definition, which layer(s) it primarily engages, relevant patterns, related terms. Not a glossary. A vocabulary that maps the terrain and makes relationships visible.

**The provocation this work carries:** A lexicon can calcify language at precisely the moment language is contested terrain (Provocation P5). The document should include an explicit semantic audit protocol — a mechanism for revisiting definitions as their use shifts and flagging when terms have been captured or displaced.

---

### Review Moment 1

Before beginning Stage 2: stop and read the Stack against what has changed in conditions. Update STATE.md. Ask:
- What did Stage 1 work reveal that wasn't anticipated?
- Which provocation has become more or less acute?
- Has the sequencing of Stage 2 held up, or should it be revised?

The answer to these questions determines Stage 2's actual shape. The plan below is the current best thinking, not a commitment.

## Stage 2: Extension

*The grounded work enables the structural work.*

### Module: Epistemic Coordination & Distributed Intelligence

This module addresses the shift from information scarcity to orientation scarcity. The scarce resource in the current environment is not data but the capacity to integrate, interpret, and act on it. Authority, in this frame, follows integration capacity — not control of information, but the ability to make it coherent across distributed actors.

**Work to do:**
- Write the module document: orientation as design object, three-layer authority model (operational/epistemic/participatory), governance architecture for multi-agent systems
- Develop verification protocols for distributed sensemaking: what counts as verification in systems without central authority?
- Articulate the epistemic workshop model: distributed sensemaking nodes with explicit open editorial practices and visible reasoning
- Address agent governance directly: how is authority delegated to non-human agents in epistemic systems? This module is the bridge into Stage 3 agentic operationalization and should be written with that in mind.

**Provocations this module must sit with:** P1 (technical opacity masking power), P3 (expertise paradox), P5 (semantic collapse). Epistemic systems are particularly vulnerable to all three. The module should engage each explicitly rather than assuming the Stack's legibility principles are sufficient as-stated. A system can be procedurally transparent while being epistemically inaccessible. The module needs language for both.

---

### Module: Commons & Institutional Governance

The institutional design counterpart to Care Infrastructure. Where Care focuses on the relational and the operational, Commons focuses on the structural: how shared resources are governed, how benefit is distributed, how democratic control is maintained as conditions change.

**Work to do:**
- Write the module document: adaptive commons characteristics, community capital pooling mechanisms, multi-stakeholder governance design
- Develop transition mechanics: how does a resource move from private or state control to commons governance? This is the highest-leverage gap across all five modules — the corpus diagnoses failure but is less explicit about how alternatives emerge without being crushed or co-opted.
- Articulate the distinction between state provision and self-governance: these are often confused, and the confusion matters for both design and legitimacy.
- Address scale explicitly: how does commons governance coordinate beyond intimate scale without recreating the centralized authority it was designed to replace?

**Provocation this module must sit with:** P6 (private authority beyond formal contestation). Commons governance is often proposed as the answer to private authority. The module needs to address when and why that answer works, when it doesn't, and what the Stack's current tools can and cannot reach.

---

### Vocabulary Consolidation

With Stage 1 lexicon complete, Stage 2 deepens and extends:
- Integrate module-specific vocabulary from Food Power and Care Infrastructure modules
- Add concepts that emerged from pattern stress-testing and the three new patterns
- Begin cross-referencing: which concepts appear in multiple modules? How do meanings shift across contexts?
- Run the first semantic audit: which Stage 1 terms have been used in ways that diverge from their definitions? Revise or flag.

---

### Pattern Maturation: Tier 2 Stress-Testing

**Care vs. Coercion**, **Platform Capture**, **Obedience in Advance**, and **Institutional Weaponization** are Tier 2 patterns. Stage 2 work:
- Identify one concrete recent case for each pattern that either confirms or strains the pattern's description
- Where the case strains: revise the pattern, not the case. Patterns that cannot be falsified are not patterns — they are ideology.
- Document which provocations each pattern most directly engages. Make those connections explicit in the pattern text.

---

### New Patterns: From Corpus Gaps

The AUDIT.md identifies taxonomy gaps that point toward unwritten patterns. Two belong in Stage 2:

**Semantic Capture** — The systematic colonization of governance vocabulary by technical or market language. Distinct from Epistemic Warfare in that it targets the vocabulary itself, not the information environment. "Freedom" becomes a market transaction; "community" becomes speculation. The terrain of contestation narrows before any formal decision is made. Directly engages Provocation P5.

**Care Infrastructure Failure** — The dynamic where care-based systems collapse not because their design was wrong but because material conditions have stripped the capacity that would make them work. The system demands care it has eliminated. Provocation P8 operationalized as a recognizable pattern — one that both diagnoses failure and points toward what would need to change.

---

### Review Moment 2

Before beginning Stage 3: reassess the full roadmap. Ask:
- What has Stage 2 clarified about provocation resolution — particularly P3 (expertise paradox) and P6 (private authority)?
- Has the agentic operationalization question become more urgent than Stage 3 placement assumed? If so, pull it forward.
- Are there module candidates that conditions have made more urgent?

The roadmap is a living document. Stage 2 review is the moment to significantly revise Stage 3 if the work has revealed what wasn't visible at the start.

## Stage 3: Operationalization

*The framework becomes usable.*

### Module: Distributed Resistance & Tactical Coordination

The most practically urgent module. Also the least amenable to designed experimentation — distributed resistance emerges from conditions, not from documents. Stage 3 placement is not because it matters less but because it requires the other modules as foundation. Epistemic coordination, commons governance, and care infrastructure are its enabling conditions.

**Work to do:**
- Write the module document: operating principles, tactical methods, the harmony principle (coordination-without-uniformity as the design target, not consensus)
- Address the hardest open question directly: how do distributed systems persist beyond crisis? Most documented examples are crisis-driven. The module should name what is known, what is unknown, and what would need to be true for persistence to be possible.
- Address infiltration and sustained repression: the module cannot treat adversarial conditions as exceptional. Systems designed only for favorable conditions are not resilient systems.

**Provocation this module must sit with:** P4 (distributed systems cannot always move fast enough in crises). This module lives inside that provocation. It should not resolve it — it should develop the best current thinking on when and how distributed coordination can move fast enough, and when it cannot. Pre-committing to decision architectures that specify when speed overrides contestation — and what recovery looks like afterward — is the direction, not a solution.

### Agentic Operationalization

The Stack was designed with a dual-use audience: humans and automated systems. In Stage 3, automated systems are not only reading the Stack — they are enacting it. This is no longer anticipated future work.

This is not a module. It is a layer of the framework that cuts across everything.

**Questions to address:**

*Delegation:* How is authority delegated to non-human agents? What is the minimal governance architecture for an agentic system operating within Stack principles? The three-layer authority model from the Epistemic Coordination module (operational/epistemic/participatory) is a candidate structure — but it was designed for human actors. What changes?

*Contestability at machine speed:* How does contestability function when decisions are made faster than human deliberation allows? Can post-hoc contestation remain meaningful, or does it become ritual? What procedural structures preserve substantive contestability even when procedural contestation is impossible in real time?

*Legibility of automated reasoning:* What does "power without legibility is unacceptable" mean for a system whose reasoning is a probability distribution? Technical legibility (you can see the output) does not equal epistemic legibility (you can meaningfully challenge the process). The distinction developed in Provocation P1 becomes foundational here.

*Agent ecosystem governance:* When multiple agents operate in the same governance environment, who governs the ecosystem? How is authority over the agents themselves contested?

**Output:** An agentic governance annex — a cross-cutting document that maps how each Stack layer applies to agentic systems and what new design requirements emerge. This is not a standalone document about AI governance. It is an extension of the Stack's existing architecture into agentic conditions.

This work intersects Provocations P1, P3, P5, and P6. It will not resolve any of them. It should name where the Stack's principles are sufficient, where they require adaptation, and where the framework has genuine gaps that cannot be papered over.

---

### Public and Practitioner Interface

The Stack is currently legible to people who are already inside it. Stage 3 makes it legible to people who are not.

**Work to do:**

*Entry document* — A 2,000-word document that a careful first-time reader could use to orient themselves without reading everything else first. Not a summary of `00-overview.md` — a different register, for a different reader, with a different starting question.

*Case study format* — A template for applying the Stack to a specific situation: a governance failure, a design challenge, a coordination problem. The format should make the diagnostic work legible — which layers are engaged, what patterns are present, what provocations are alive in the situation. One completed case study as proof-of-concept.

*Design toolkit* — A working document for practitioners designing or governing a specific system. Not prescriptive. Structured enough to be used without prior Stack knowledge. Entry point for the modules most relevant to the practitioner's domain.

*Pattern quick-reference* — A single document listing all patterns with 1–2 sentence descriptions and their primary layer engagements. For rapid orientation when reading or writing, not for deep engagement.

### Review Moment 3

End-of-roadmap review. Not a conclusion — a reorientation. What has the project learned that should reshape the next version of this document? What changed in conditions that the roadmap did not anticipate? Update STATE.md. Begin the next roadmap.

The project does not end. The roadmap does.

## Provocation Work: Explicit Accounting

The eight provocations from `PROVOCATIONS.md` are not problems to be solved. They are conditions the roadmap must be honest about. Here is the current accounting of their status:

| Provocation | Status | Primary Stage |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** Legibility masking power | Sustained development — lexicon + module work will surface this repeatedly; needs explicit meta-linguistic protocols across all stages | Ongoing |
| **P2** Care-based authority resisting contestation | Near-term addressable — Care module must develop the distinction between care-as-support and care-as-authority | Stage 1 |
| **P3** Expertise paradox | Sustained development — Epistemic Coordination module should develop "permeable expertise" framework; no clean resolution expected | Stage 2 |
| **P4** Resilience vs. speed | Permanent design condition — the Stack needs to institutionalize the trade-off, not resolve it; crisis protocols and decision pre-commitment are the direction | Ongoing |
| **P5** Semantic collapse | Sustained development — lexicon work is the primary response; must include semantic audit protocols rather than treating definitions as stable | Stages 1–2 |
| **P6** Private authority beyond contestation | Sustained development — Commons module and agentic annex will engage this; no resolution anticipated within this roadmap's scope | Stages 2–3 |
| **P7** Material base as authority mechanism | Near-term addressable — Food Power module should explicitly elevate Layer 1 from precondition to contested political terrain | Stage 1 |
| **P8** Care capacity inversion | Permanent design condition — cannot be designed away; must be named as a prerequisite constraint in every module | Ongoing |

## Genuine Uncertainty: What the Roadmap Does Not Resolve

Following the Stack's own principles, here is what is genuinely uncertain in this document:

**On sequencing:** The case for starting with Food Power and Care Infrastructure is strong — deepest design thinking, most concrete operational paths. But the case for starting with the lexicon is also real: without shared vocabulary, module work will generate terms that need to be reconciled later. A future version of this roadmap may reverse the order.

**On the pattern library:** The three unwritten patterns are the most clearly indicated additions. Whether they belong in Stage 1 or whether other candidates from the corpus deserve priority is not settled. The pattern library should be treated as a living set, not a completion queue.

**On agentic operationalization:** Stage 3 placement may be wrong. The governance questions around agentic systems are already live — they are not waiting for Stages 1 and 2 to complete. A reasonable alternative is that agentic operationalization should run in parallel from Stage 1. The placement here reflects a judgment that the framework needs to be more grounded before those questions can be addressed well. That judgment should be revisited at Review Moment 1.

**On lexicon scope:** 50–75 terms for Stage 1 may be too many. The risk of premature vocabulary closure is real (Provocation P5). Fewer terms, more carefully defined, with explicit semantic audit trails, may be better than a comprehensive first pass.

**On module outputs:** The roadmap describes the work without specifying its form. Some outputs will be documents; some will be experimental designs; some will be revised patterns; some will be conversations. The form should follow the work, not precede it.

---

## What This Roadmap Is Not

- A complete build plan with timelines and owners. Timelines belong in working documents, not in a document meant to survive changing conditions.
- A content calendar. Signals, newsletter, and extractions continue on their own logic, feeding the framework as they always have.
- A resolution of the provocations. The roadmap sits with them, names them accurately, and builds toward the framework capacity to navigate them — not dissolve them.
- Finished. Return to this document when conditions change. Revise sequencing when evidence suggests a different order. Treat it as a compass, not a contract.
