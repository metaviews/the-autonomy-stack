## Executive Summary

The extraction corpus reveals a clear three-stage progression from Crisis Detection (Oct–Dec 2024) through Institutional Failure (Jan–Jun 2025) to Alternative Construction (Jul 2025–Mar 2026). The recent phase shows mature conceptual development and early design thinking across five distinct areas that meet the criteria for module candidacy: sufficient depth, internal coherence, analytical rigor, and actionable design potential.

## Module Candidate 1: Food Power & Agricultural Sovereignty

**Summary**
A comprehensive governance framework for treating domestic food systems as strategic infrastructure combining production, logistics, and market design. This module has evolved from analysis of food as a legitimacy metric through diagnosis of architectural failures to concrete design proposals. The thinking spans all six Stack layers. Recent work includes explicit design proposals: the "10-Point Food Power Program" (2025-12-17), community resource pooling frameworks (2026-03-10), and operational models distinguishing baseline stability from entrepreneurial upside.

**Evidence base**
- *Concept-dense issues:* Why Food Should Be Free and Profitable (2025-12-14), Hidden Architecture of Food (2025-12-15), Intelligence Layer of Food (2025-12-16), How Food Power Gets Built (2025-12-17)
- *Foundational analysis:* Farm Labor, Prison Labor, Food Insecurity (2025-01-28), Against the Herd (2025-05-12)
- *Deployment cases:* First Nations and Next Generation Farmers (2026-03-10), Goats of Toronto (2025-08-05)
- *Recurring concepts:* Food as infrastructure vs. market commodity; labor capacity stabilization; data enclosure/opening; risk vs. upside separation; agricultural literacy as market knowledge

**Layer engagement**
- Layer 1: Production logistics, storage redundancy, biological systems integration
- Layer 2: Community pooling models, cooperative governance, labor protection frameworks
- Layer 3: Agricultural data as public infrastructure, literacy as strategic knowledge
- Layer 4: Risk allocation rules, public-private mandate separation
- Layer 5: Consumer/producer literacy, data access and interpretation
- Layer 6: Labor capacity maintenance, worker safety, dignity-centered design

**Current state of development**
Worked out: failure mode diagnosis (logistics over production, private data enclosure, labor precarity as design feature). Design of dual-track systems separating domestic security from export competition. Risk allocation as governance principle.

Remaining open: institutional implementation; multi-region coordination; transition from consolidated supply chains; climate adaptation integration.

**What structured experimentation might look like**
Regional food security system with: (1) guaranteed local production quotas and storage capacity, (2) worker capacity agreements with explicit wage/condition standards, (3) open data protocols for agricultural intelligence, (4) community pooling mechanisms for entry capital/land access, (5) dual-mandate governance (domestic stability + export innovation).

**Gaps and risks**
Global trade agreement integration; climate volatility response; Indigenous land sovereignty relation to governance; risk of technocratic enclosure; new enclosure around "public" agricultural data.

## Module Candidate 2: Epistemic Coordination & Distributed Intelligence

**Summary**
A framework for maintaining shared reality and enabling coherent action across distributed human and machine actors in conditions of information overabundance and systematic epistemic warfare. This module addresses the shift from information scarcity to orientation scarcity, distinguishes between knowledge accumulation and authority-generating literacy, and proposes governance architectures for multi-agent systems. Core insight: authority in an agentic era follows integration capacity, not information control.

**Evidence base**
- *Concept-dense issues:* Democracy in the Age of Epistemic War (2026-03-01), Notes from the Epistemic Workshop (2026-03-05), Ecology of Intelligence (2026-03-06)
- *Foundational analysis:* Intelligence Layer of Food (2025-12-16), Airports Are Borders (2025-08-20)
- *Recurring concepts:* Orientation as scarce resource; transparency vs. participation; systems literacy for defense; harmony as coordination; authority through integration capacity

**Layer engagement**
- Layer 3: Orientation as design object; automated bureaucratic memory; algorithmic transparency and contestation
- Layer 4: Three-layer authority model (operational, epistemic, participatory); authority through integration
- Layer 5: Collaborative knowledge production; non-hierarchical channels; verification and validation protocols
- Layer 6: Systems literacy as civic infrastructure; human judgment preservation

**Current state of development**
Worked out: fragmentation as control mechanism; definition of literacy as systems understanding; identification of orientation as scarce resource; three-layer authority model for agent ecosystems.

Remaining open: protocols for multi-agent coordination at scale; preventing algorithmic collapse to engagement; epistemic stewardship forms; contestation mechanisms for authority figures.

**What structured experimentation might look like**
Epistemic workshop with: (1) distributed sensemaking nodes (conversations, briefs, salons, agentic analysis), (2) explicit verification protocols, (3) open editorial practices with visible reasoning, (4) agent governance separating operational/epistemic/participatory layers, (5) literacy-building as core function.

**Gaps and risks**
Scalability of human judgment; preventing cognitive authority concentration; maintaining productive disagreement; risk of new curator power; systems literacy becoming exclusionary.

## Module Candidate 3: Care-Based Infrastructure Design

**Summary**
A governance framework treating essential systems (food, health, transport, energy) as care infrastructure rather than commodities, with authority measured by responsibility under constraint rather than responsiveness on demand. Key development: reframing transactional governance failure as opportunity for care-based models, establishing care as a design condition, and developing "shared endurance" as the operational principle.

**Evidence base**
- *Concept-dense issues:* From Taxpayers to Caretakers (2026-02-04), Resurgent Commons (2025-04-10), Mutual Aid in LA (2025-01-14)
- *Foundational analysis:* We Should Hire More Social Workers (2024-12-07), Metabolic Rift and LA Fires (2025-01-11)
- *Recurring concepts:* Care vs. transaction; failure management as design; shared endurance; dignity-centered; relational authority; grief as political category

**Layer engagement**
- Layer 1: Care infrastructure as material substrate; redundancy and slack as design features
- Layer 2: Community-based services; tripartite ownership; cooperative governance
- Layer 4: Authority through responsibility not responsiveness; legitimacy through visible effort; decisions acknowledging loss
- Layer 6: Care ethics as design principle; relational capacity; dignity-centered metrics

**Current state of development**
Worked out: critique of transactional governance; definition of care infrastructure as failure management; authority redefined around responsibility; institutional models (cooperative governance, care-centered response).

Remaining open: scaling care relationships; preventing care from becoming paternalism; financing redundancy-based systems; care-centered metrics.

**What structured experimentation might look like**
Regional care infrastructure with: (1) essential services governed by care ethics not profit metrics, (2) explicit redundancy budgets, (3) visible constraint and loss acknowledgment, (4) multi-stakeholder governance, (5) relational accountability structures.

**Gaps and risks**
Preventative vs. curative balance; market integration; risk of care becoming paternalism; protection as coercion; romanticization of relational governance.

## Module Candidate 4: Commons & Institutional Governance

**Summary**
A framework for building shared-resource governance systems that adapt to contemporary complexity while preserving democratic control, equitable distribution, and community accountability. Key development: moving from defensive commons protection to active construction, establishing community resource pooling as an answer to capital barriers, and distinguishing state provision from self-governance.

**Evidence base**
- *Concept-dense issues:* Resurgent Commons (2025-04-10), First Nations and Next Generation Farmers (2026-03-10), Mutual Aid in LA (2025-01-14)
- *Foundational analysis:* Against the Herd (2025-05-12), Cryptocurrency Propaganda (2024-11-13)
- *Recurring concepts:* Tripartite ownership; community resource pooling; commons as adaptive; care-centric governance; collective learning

**Layer engagement**
- Layer 1: Common-pool resources; bio-infrastructure; shared logistics
- Layer 2: Democratic governance; benefit distribution; conflict resolution; adaptation
- Layer 4: Democratic participation; transparency; integrated knowledge
- Layer 5: Shared knowledge; learning systems; data commons
- Layer 6: Relational capacity; labor conditions; dignity-centered design

**Current state of development**
Worked out: multi-stakeholder governance models; community capital pooling; adaptive commons characteristics; integration with disaster response and care.

Remaining open: scale coordination; market interface; technological governance; knowledge integration across diversity.

**What structured experimentation might look like**
Commons governance for a critical resource with: (1) multi-stakeholder governance, (2) explicit benefit distribution, (3) regular adaptation procedures, (4) community capital pooling, (5) diverse knowledge integration, (6) transparency and contestability, (7) care-centered decisions.

**Gaps and risks**
Heterogeneity management; power dynamics at scale; external pressure; long-term persistence; risk of exclusionary community membership; state responsibility displacement.

## Module Candidate 5: Distributed Resistance & Tactical Coordination

**Summary**
A framework for building resistance movements without centralized command that remain responsive to local conditions and coordinate across distributed actors. Key developments: informal networks scale faster than bureaucracies in crises; tactical use of infrastructure glitches; livestream intelligence integration; harmony as coordination principle. Grounded in practical case analysis rather than abstract theory.

**Evidence base**
- *Concept-dense issues:* Airports Are Borders (2025-08-20), Democracy in the Age of Epistemic War (2026-03-01), Mutual Aid in LA (2025-01-14)
- *Foundational analysis:* Against the Herd (2025-05-12), Every Block Has a Heartbeat (2025-08-15)
- *Pattern bases:* Distributed Resistance, Epistemic Warfare
- *Recurring concepts:* Decentralized decision-making; livestream awareness; tactical timing; cognitive authority; local knowledge; harmony as coordination

**Layer engagement**
- Layer 1: Infrastructure as tactical surface; parallel systems; resource redistribution
- Layer 2: Non-hierarchical organization; consensus decision-making; knowledge sharing
- Layer 3: Opacity/legibility as tactics; counter-surveillance; awareness
- Layer 4: Non-command coordination; situational authority; contestable decisions; rapid adaptation
- Layer 5: Distributed sensing; verification without centralization; shared awareness
- Layer 6: Care-based accountability; support structures; dignity preservation

**Current state of development**
Worked out: operating principles (distributed leadership, consensus, local agency); tactical methods (infrastructure disruption, time-buying); technical-social intelligence integration; speed advantage during crises.

Remaining open: scaling without centralization; knowledge preservation; decision quality; persistence beyond crisis; institutionalization of success.

**What structured experimentation might look like**
Multi-issue coordination network with: (1) autonomous local chapters, (2) common protocols, (3) distributed intelligence, (4) tactical coordination, (5) explicit refusal of central command, (6) care-based support, (7) cross-node learning mechanisms.

**Gaps and risks**
Reliability under sustained repression; infiltration resistance; long-term persistence; institutional memory; risk of burnout; state adaptation and surveillance scaling.

## Cross-Module Patterns

**Care as cross-cutting principle**
All five modules engage care (relational accountability, capacity maintenance, dignity) as design condition. Layer 6 moves from underrepresented in the early corpus to central in mature thinking.

**Authority through capacity, not control**
Across modules, authority derives from integration capacity (epistemic), care capacity (infrastructure), stewardship (commons), coordinated intelligence (resistance), or production capacity (food) — legitimated through competence and responsibility rather than coercion.

**Transparency vs. participation tension**
Every module distinguishes legibility from genuine participation. Legibility without participation becomes surveillance. Participation without transparency becomes clientelism. Integration of both is the design challenge.

**Scale without centralization**
Common challenge across all five: coordinate beyond intimate scale without recreating centralized authority. Solutions include explicit protocols, federated authority, harmony through alignment-not-uniformity, rapid adaptive capacity.

**Data as infrastructure, not product**
Food systems, epistemic coordination, commons, and resistance all require treating data as shared infrastructure rather than proprietary asset.

## Recommended Sequencing

**Phase 1 (Immediate): Food Power + Care Infrastructure**
Deepest design thinking; clearest operational paths; grounded in concrete systems; address urgent legitimacy crises.

**Phase 2 (~6 months): Epistemic Coordination + Commons Governance**
Support Phase 1 modules; enable distributed decision-making; provide institutional forms for care infrastructure.

**Phase 3 (~12 months): Distributed Resistance**
Grounded in practice; less explicit design architecture; needs other modules as foundation; urgently practical.

## Key Unresolved Tensions Across All Modules

**Authority-contestability paradox**
How to build systems with sufficient authority to coordinate and absorb risk while maintaining contestability and preventing crystallization into domination?

**Scale-intimacy trade-off**
Care, commons, and resistance work best at intimate scale. How to scale without losing relational quality?

**Technical vs. social systems integration**
How to integrate algorithmic systems (scalable, information-preserving) with relational systems (care-preserving, contestable) without colonization?

**Transition paths**
How do modules emerge from current systems without being crushed, co-opted, or burned out? The corpus diagnoses failure but is less explicit about transition mechanics. Highest-leverage area for next-phase development.
