# Design Toolkit

*A working document for practitioners designing, governing, or diagnosing a specific system.*

This toolkit is organized as questions — not a checklist to satisfy but a structure for thinking through what a system requires, what it might miss, and where it is most likely to fail. Work through the sections relevant to your situation. Not all questions will apply.

For each section, identify: what the design currently provides, what it assumes, and where the design is silent. Silence is not neutral — it usually means the issue will be resolved by whoever has the most power when it arises.

## Layer 1: Material Base

*What material conditions does your system require that may not currently exist?*

**Dependency mapping**
- What material inputs does this system require to function? (Infrastructure, energy, physical space, food security for participants)
- Who controls those inputs? On what terms?
- What happens if those inputs are disrupted, priced out of reach, or withheld?

**Coercion through material leverage**
- Does this system create material dependencies that concentrate power over participants?
- Can participants be pressured through control of their material conditions rather than through the system itself?
- Who cannot afford to exit? Is that design or accident?

**Logistics and supply**
- Are critical supply chains owned or controlled by participants, or by external actors whose interests may diverge?
- What are the single points of failure in the material infrastructure this system depends on?

**Labor and care capacity**
- What does this system require from the people who maintain it? Are those requirements sustainable over time?
- Are those people materially secure enough to do the work without being coerced by precarity?

*Patterns to check: [Material Base Sovereignty](../patterns/material-base-sovereignty.md), [Care Infrastructure Failure](../patterns/care-infrastructure-failure.md), [Commons Destruction](../patterns/commons-destruction.md)*

## Layer 2: Commons & Institutions

*What structures sustain collective agency in this system — and what makes them vulnerable?*

**Institutional form**
- What is the governing structure? Who has formal authority and through what mechanism?
- Does the form match the function? (A cooperative structure applied to a capitalist extraction logic will not hold.)
- Who is accountable to whom, and what are the enforcement mechanisms?

**Capture risk**
- Who has the most to gain from capturing this institution?
- What are the points of entry for capture? (Board composition, funding dependence, procurement rules, regulatory relationships)
- What design elements make capture more or less difficult?

**Commons governance**
- If this system manages shared resources, are Ostrom's conditions in place? (Defined membership, proportional rules, collective choice arrangements, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution, external recognition)
- How are rules changed? Who has standing to initiate revision?
- How are commons distinguished from private goods within the system?

**Scale and coordination**
- At what scale does the governance structure stop working?
- How does coordination happen across nodes or units without recreating central authority?
- What happens when component institutions have conflicting interests?

**Institutional memory**
- How does the system preserve institutional knowledge when key participants leave?
- What prevents the rules from being gradually reinterpreted by whoever has current power?

*Patterns to check: [Platform Capture](../patterns/platform-capture.md), [Commons Destruction](../patterns/commons-destruction.md), [Institutional Weaponization](../patterns/institutional-weaponization.md), [Procedural Entombment](../patterns/procedural-entombment.md), [Collective Leadership Architecture](../patterns/collective-leadership-architecture.md)*

## Layer 3: Legibility & Metrics

*Who can see what — and who can contest it?*

**Output legibility**
- What does this system produce, and can participants see what was produced?
- Who has access to the outputs? On what terms? At what cost?
- What is invisible to participants that would be relevant to their decisions?

**Reasoning legibility**
- Can participants trace why a decision was made, not just what was decided?
- Is the reasoning accessible to non-specialists, or only to those with technical expertise?
- Distinguish: *technical legibility* (the mechanism can be seen) vs. *epistemic legibility* (the mechanism can be understood and challenged by those affected).

**Metrics and optimization**
- What does this system measure? What does it optimize for?
- What is the relationship between the metric and the underlying value it is supposed to represent? Where does that relationship break down?
- Who benefits from the current measurement choices? Who is made invisible by them?
- What behaviors does the metric incentivize that were not intended?

**Vocabulary**
- What terms does this system use that have contested or shifting meanings?
- Has the vocabulary been inherited from a domain with different assumptions? (Technical language applied to social systems; market language applied to care systems)
- Who set the terms of discussion, and whose assumptions do those terms carry?

**Contesting what is seen**
- If a participant believes the system is producing wrong outputs or using wrong metrics, what is the pathway for challenge?
- Is that pathway accessible to participants without specialized expertise?

*Patterns to check: [Algorithmic Authority](../patterns/algorithmic-authority.md), [Manufactured Ambiguity](../patterns/manufactured-ambiguity.md), [Semantic Capture](../patterns/semantic-capture.md), [Temporal Capture](../patterns/temporal-capture.md)*

## Layer 4: Decision Systems & Authority

*How are binding decisions made — and can they be contested, revised, or reversed?*

**Decision authority**
- Who makes binding decisions? By what process?
- What decisions cannot be made without broader participation?
- Are decision-making rules themselves subject to revision? By whom?

**Contestability**
- How can a participant challenge a decision they believe is wrong?
- Is that challenge pathway procedurally available or practically accessible? (The distinction matters: a process that exists but requires resources most participants lack is not meaningfully contestable.)
- What is the realistic timeline for a challenge to produce effect? Is that timeline compatible with the speed at which consequences occur?

**Reversibility**
- Which decisions can be reversed if they turn out to be wrong?
- Which decisions produce irreversible consequences? Are those decisions subject to higher scrutiny and broader participation?
- Is there an active design commitment to preserving revisability, or is permanence the default?

**Delegation**
- What authority has been delegated to automated systems, subcommittees, or external actors?
- Is that delegation explicit and bounded, or has it emerged through practice without formal authorization?
- What happens when delegated authority conflicts with participant interests? Who has standing to recall the delegation?

**Procedural dynamics**
- Does process function as a genuine accountability mechanism or as a shield against substantive challenge?
- Are procedures stable enough to be relied on but revisable enough to not become entrenched?

*Patterns to check: [Proceduralism as Shield](../patterns/proceduralism-as-shield.md), [Procedural Entombment](../patterns/procedural-entombment.md), [Legitimacy Drift](../patterns/legitimacy-drift.md), [Algorithmic Authority](../patterns/algorithmic-authority.md), [Strategic Ambiguity](../patterns/strategic-ambiguity.md), [Institutional Weaponization](../patterns/institutional-weaponization.md)*

## Layer 5: Knowledge & Intelligence

*How does knowledge form in this system, and does it remain open to revision?*

**Knowledge production**
- How does this system generate the knowledge it acts on?
- Who has standing to contribute? Who is systematically excluded from the knowledge-formation process?
- What verification standards apply? Are those standards visible and contestable?

**Authority and expertise**
- Where does epistemic authority — the power to say what counts as knowledge — reside?
- Is that authority earned through visible reasoning or granted through position?
- Can participants with relevant local or lived knowledge contest the outputs of credentialed expertise?

**Revision mechanisms**
- How does the system update when its current knowledge is wrong?
- What is the process for incorporating evidence that contradicts the prevailing account?
- What signals indicate that current knowledge needs revision, and who is positioned to see them?

**Adversarial conditions**
- Is this system designed to function in an environment where some actors have incentives to corrupt its knowledge base?
- How are attempts to manufacture consensus or capture vocabulary detected and resisted?
- What shared vocabulary does this system require, and how is it protected from semantic drift or capture?

*Patterns to check: [Epistemic Warfare](../patterns/epistemic-warfare.md), [Semantic Capture](../patterns/semantic-capture.md), [Manufactured Ambiguity](../patterns/manufactured-ambiguity.md), [Algorithmic Authority](../patterns/algorithmic-authority.md)*

## Layer 6: Human Capacity & Care

*What relational conditions does this system require — and does it sustain or deplete them?*

**Care substrate**
- What relational work does this system depend on? (Trust-building, sustained engagement, conflict processing, emotional labor)
- Is that work visible and resourced, or invisible and assumed?
- Who bears the care burden? Is that distribution equitable or concentrated?

**Sustainability**
- What does sustained participation cost participants? Time, emotional labor, financial cost, risk?
- Is the cost sustainable over a timeline longer than the initial mobilization?
- What happens to the system when key participants burn out?

**Autonomy and dependency**
- Does participation in this system support or reduce the capacity for independent action?
- Are participants more or less able to act autonomously after extended engagement than before?
- What dependencies does the system create between participants and the system itself?

**Control and coercion**
- What happens when care becomes leverage? When the system's provision of support creates obligation that forecloses dissent?
- Distinguish: *care as support* (resource provision that increases capacity) vs. *care as authority* (decision-making power derived from relational position).
- Is there a meaningful pathway to contest care-based authority without losing access to care?

**Resistance to suppression**
- If the system comes under external pressure, what relational conditions are required to sustain it?
- Is the relational infrastructure in place before pressure arrives, or is it assumed to emerge in response to crisis?
- What care capacity is required for collective action under sustained adversarial conditions?

*Patterns to check: [Care vs. Coercion](../patterns/care-vs-coercion.md), [Obedience in Advance](../patterns/obedience-in-advance.md), [Care Infrastructure Failure](../patterns/care-infrastructure-failure.md), [Distributed Resistance](../patterns/distributed-resistance.md), [Internalized Policing](../patterns/internal-policing.md)*

## Cross-Layer Questions

These questions require looking across multiple layers simultaneously.

**Failure propagation**
- If this system fails in Layer 1 (material), how quickly does that failure reach Layer 6 (care capacity)? What is the propagation path?
- Which layer is most fragile? Does that fragility cascade?
- What layers are currently absorbing stress from failures elsewhere?

**Power concentration**
- Where is authority concentrated in this system? Across how many layers does that concentration extend?
- Is there a single actor that controls material inputs (L1), institutional form (L2), measurement (L3), decisions (L4), knowledge production (L5), and care provision (L6)? If so, you have a capture condition, not a governance system.

**Agentic systems**
- If automated systems are making or informing decisions in this design, apply the checks from each layer specifically to those systems:
  - L3: Can participants understand and contest the reasoning of automated decisions?
  - L4: Are automated decisions reversible? Is the delegation explicit and bounded?
  - L5: What epistemic authority have automated systems accumulated through use? Is it monitored?
  - L6: What human oversight role exists, and does it have substantive authority or only formal presence?
- See the [Agentic Governance Annex](agentic-governance-annex.md) for the full cross-layer analysis.

**Provocation check**
Before finalizing a design, read [PROVOCATIONS.md](PROVOCATIONS.md) and ask:
- P1: Is the system technically legible but epistemically inaccessible?
- P2: Does care-based authority in this system resist legitimate contestation?
- P3: Does expertise in this system translate into governance authority that cannot be challenged by non-experts?
- P4: Can this system move fast enough in genuine crises? What is the speed-contestability trade-off?
- P5: Is the vocabulary this system uses stable, or is it contested terrain?
- P6: Does this system create private authority that operates beyond formal contestation mechanisms?
- P7: Does control of material inputs in this system translate into political constraint?
- P8: Does this system generate demand for care capacity at the same rate it depletes it?

## Using This Toolkit in Practice

**Diagnostic mode:** You have a system that is failing or behaving in unexpected ways. Move through the layers as a diagnostic: which questions are hardest to answer? Where is the design silent? The silences will usually locate the failure.

**Design mode:** You are building something new. Work through every layer before finalizing the design. The questions you cannot answer are the design requirements you have not yet specified.

**Review mode:** A system has been operating for some time. Run the toolkit as an audit. Focus on what has changed since the design was set: which assumptions are no longer true? Which answers have shifted?

This toolkit is not exhaustive. It is a starting structure, not a complete process. The Stack's pattern library and modules provide deeper analysis for specific domains and dynamics.
