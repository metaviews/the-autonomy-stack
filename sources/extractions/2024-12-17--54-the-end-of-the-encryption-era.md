### SIGNAL
**Google Willow Chip Signals Quantum Scaling Breakthrough**

Google’s Willow chip marks a shift from theoretical quantum computing to reliable, scalable hardware. It demonstrates exponential error reduction and performs complex tasks in minutes rather than eons, signaling that the technological threat to digital security is approaching immediate practical reality rather than remaining speculative. This shifts the context from "whether" quantum breaks encryption to "when".

### PATTERN CANDIDATE
**Migration Gaps Create Interim Vulnerability For Data Security**

This pattern describes a period of systemic fragility during urgent protocol transitions. It occurs when legacy systems must operate alongside migrating security standards without full interoperability. The forces driving it include the lag in global coordination and the speed of technological obsolescence. The consequence is that data encrypted today with old standards becomes vulnerable to decryption in the future ("harvest now, decrypt later"). Ignoring this gap creates a time-bomb for institutional trust; engaging with it requires accelerated coordination and interim protections, slowing down migration to maintain safety.

Source: Section "The Quantum Threat to Encryption" regarding "harvested now and decrypted later" and the decade-long transition window.

### STACK MATERIAL
**Layer 4 and 3: Cryptography Anchors Institutional Authority**

Current layers of Decision Systems (Layer 4) depend entirely on the Material Base (Layer 1) security protocols (cryptography). This extends the Stack's framing by identifying that legitimacy relies on *algorithmic hardness* rather than human oversight or institutional history. If the material layer of encryption fails, Legibility (Layer 3) becomes inaccessible to unauthorized parties, but Authority (Layer 4) collapses without human verification mechanisms to replace them. This challenges the assumption that governance layers can remain static while technical infrastructure updates.

Source: Section "Implications for Authority" on erosion of financial systems and democratic governance due to encryption obsolescence.

### CONCEPT
**Cryptographic Agility Means Rapid Protocol Adaptation Speed**

A measure of a system's capacity to switch between security standards without architectural overhaul. Unlike static security configurations, agility allows systems to respond to emerging threats (like quantum computing) immediately. It connects to Stack Principle "Resilience over Elegance" by prioritizing functional adaptability over optimized, rigid efficiency. Governance must now measure institutional "agility" as a metric of safety, not just code.

Source: Section "What Is Cryptographic Agility" regarding ability to quickly adapt to new protocols.
Cross-reference: See SIGNAL: Quantitative Scaling.

### CONCEPT
**Shor's Algorithm Breaks Classical Encryption Efficiency**

A mathematical procedure capable of factoring large numbers exponentially faster than classical algorithms. It is the theoretical underpinning for quantum decryption of RSA and ECC. In the Stack, this represents a specific *Knowledge* threat (Layer 5) that invalidates the *Material* security (Layer 1) of current layers. It must be logged as a specific failure mode that renders the "Legibility" (Layer 3) of existing encrypted networks public knowledge to an adversary.

Source: Section "The Quantum Threat to Encryption" regarding algorithms like Shor's that can crack codes using quantum mechanics.
Cross-reference: See SIGNAL: Google Willow Chip.

### PROVCATION
**Trust Collapse Forces Decentralization Over Digital Upgrades**

The text suggests existing centralized systems may not survive the quantum transition, favoring "localized networks" and "community-driven protocols". This challenges the Stack's current assumption that authority can be defended through better technical standards within centralized institutions. It proposes that authority *must* degrade to the human layer (Layer 6) and physical locality if digital legibility is compromised. It complicates the principle "power without legibility is unacceptable" by suggesting the legibility itself (encryption) is the vector of control that must be abandoned.
