## PATTERN CANDIDATE

**Asymmetry of Speed: Fast Ideology vs. Slow Institutions**
The dynamic where rapidly mobilized, well-funded ideological movements (reactionary and accelerationist) outpace the slow-moving, bureaucratic mechanisms of liberal democracy. This occurs when institutional protections like free speech and open markets are weaponized by insurgents rather than defended. Consequences include the erosion of public trust and the inability of democratic processes to adapt to technological and financial leverage before the system is hollowed out from within.

*Source note:* Observes that liberal bureaucracy "struggles to counter fast, ruthless ideological insurgencies" and that procedural safeguards are being "exploited... to dismantle the system from within."

## CONCEPT

**Broligarchy**
A governance condition merging Silicon Valley billionaires, crypto evangelists, and nationalist oligarchs. Unlike traditional plutocracy, broligarchs actively install leaders or dismantle systems rather than merely lobbying. Connects to Stack Principle "Power without legibility is unacceptable" as this class often operates through opaque corporate structures and dark funding channels to achieve political ends.

*Source note:* Derived from the text describing the fusion of "Silicon Valley billionaires, crypto evangelists, and nationalist oligarchs" who are "actively installing their preferred leaders."

**CEO Monarchism**
An ideological framework where the state is run as a corporation led by a "CEO monarch." Legitimacy is based on executive competence and capital efficiency rather than voter consent or legal due process. Connects to Stack Layer 4 decision systems and challenges the Stack principle "authority must be contestable" by substituting corporate hierarchy for democratic accountability.

*Source note:* Curtis Yarvin’s “neo-reactionary vision” advocating for "strongman rule modeled on corporate governance."

**Effective Accelerationism (e/acc)**
A movement abandoning democratic incrementalism for radical technological and political upheaval. It posits that democracy impedes progress and AI-driven transformation. Connects to Stack Layer 3 (Legibility) and Layer 6 (Human Capacity) by prioritizing technological systems over human institutional care, potentially rendering human governance secondary to algorithmic optimization.

*Source note:* Described as seeking a "post-democratic future driven by AI and sovereign digital enclaves" originating from thinkers like Nick Land.

**Left-Wing Conservatism**
A proposed future governance model blending traditional social values with economic redistribution and environmental protection. It rejects neoliberal individualism and oligarchy to protect communities from unchecked tech/corporate influence while preserving cultural continuity. Connects to Layer 2 (Commons) and Layer 6 (Care), offering an alternative to both technocracy and right-wing oligarchy.

*Source note:* Defined in the text as a movement blending values with redistribution, rejecting "neoliberal individualism and right-wing oligarchy."

## PROVOCATION

Challenges the assumption that democratic institutions can be repaired or restored to a previous state. The text argues "there is no normal to return to" and that doubling down on old liberal models (bipartisanship, status quo procedure) will ensure failure. This demands the Stack consider governance design not for restoration, but for radical replacement via "counter-offensives" that may require illiberal or non-democratic speed to survive the current threat.

*Source note:* Statement that "liberal order can no longer defend itself" and establishment instincts to "double down on the old model" are insufficient.
