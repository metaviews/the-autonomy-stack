# AUTONOMY STACK EXTRACTION CORPUS AUDIT

## Executive Summary

This audit examines 295 markdown extraction files from the Metaviews newsletter corpus (October 2024 - March 2026), analyzing a total of 1,295 categorized items across six extraction categories. The analysis reveals systematic gaps in the taxonomy, emerging themes that cross multiple issues, and significant variations in extraction depth across the corpus.

**Key Findings:**
- 214 files contain complete extractions; 81 files are sparse or incomplete
- 138 files (47%) surface items classified as OTHER, indicating taxonomy gaps
- Layer 4 (Decision Systems & Authority) dominates Stack Material discussions
- Temporal arc shows shift from authority critique to material/care-based governance models
- Recurring concepts cluster around legitimacy crises, algorithmic authority, and distributed resistance patterns

## 2. OTHER ITEMS IN FULL

The 138 files with OTHER sections reveal consistent gaps in the taxonomy. Representative items by gap category:

### A. Private Sovereign Entities / Transnational Authority
- **Source:** 2024-10-30 (Looming Legitimacy Crisis)
- **Item:** Tech Feudal Kingmaker Model — Private tech billionaires wielding state-level coercive capacity (jail threats, narrative control) without formal state authority
- **Rationale:** Autonomy Stack focuses on public governance layers; this describes unaccountable private actors with sovereign-equivalent capacity
- **Suggested Category:** New layer or subcategory: "Private/Transnational Authority"

### B. Knowledge Production / Epistemological Intervention
- **Source:** 2025-06-07 (Great Trump Depression)
- **Item:** Future-Dispatch as Governance Simulation Tool — Uses speculative fiction ("2050 dispatch") to alter present behavior regarding financial legitimacy
- **Rationale:** Resists existing taxonomy by blurring journalistic and speculative genres; functions as knowledge system intervention
- **Suggested Category:** KNOWLEDGE SIMULATION (potential Layer 5 subcategory)
- **Frequency:** Appears in 3+ files

### C. Material Constraints on Knowledge Production
- **Source:** 2025-05-12 (Against the Herd)
- **Item:** Physical labor (farming) directly constrains information production capacity
- **Rationale:** Highlights interdependency between Material Base (Layer 1) and Knowledge Production (Layer 5) that current framework treats separately
- **Suggested Category:** SYSTEM CAPACITY CONSTRAINT (cross-layer item)

### D. Semantic / Language Colonization
- **Source:** 2024-11-13 (Cryptocurrency Propaganda System)
- **Item:** Technical terms systematically replace social concepts ("freedom" → market transactions, "community" → speculation)
- **Rationale:** Operates at linguistic infrastructure level, distinct from technical systems
- **Suggested Category:** LINGUISTIC AUTHORITY (new subcategory of Layer 5)
- **Frequency:** 5+ instances across crypto, AI, and tech discourse

### E. Municipal / Local Governance Pressure Tactics
- **Source:** 2026-03-10 (First Nations and Next Generation)
- **Item:** Constituent pressure campaigns, ombudsman complaints, public naming of officials to block infrastructure privatization
- **Rationale:** Meta-governance strategies rather than authority patterns
- **Suggested Category:** GOVERNANCE RESISTANCE TACTICS

### F. Crowdsourced / Decentralized Governance Tools
- **Source:** 2024-11-22 (Tyranny of Television)
- **Item:** Decentralized long-form debate and interactive fact-checking as design pattern
- **Rationale:** Proposes implementation rather than analyzing existing power structure
- **Suggested Category:** STACK EXTENSION (proposals for design)

### G. Reflexive Authority
- **Source:** 2026-02-04 (From Taxpayers to Caretakers)
- **Item:** Meta-feedback about Stack's own discourse — "legibility over expertise" may conflict with perceived competence expectations
- **Rationale:** Self-referential; tests whether the operating system maintains perceived authority while prioritizing legibility
- **Suggested Category:** REFLEXIVE AUTHORITY (Stack's own legitimacy)

### H. Governance Skill Incompatibility
- **Source:** 2024-11-05 (New Aristocracy of Attention)
- **Item:** Influencers transitioning into elected office; persuasion/attention skills don't align with administrative governance

## 5. TEMPORAL ARC

### Early Corpus (Oct–Dec 2024): Authority Critique Era

**Dominant themes:** Algorithmic authority replacing institutional authority, legitimacy crises in democratic institutions, tech billionaires as new power brokers, media control and gatekeeping.

**Layer focus:** Heavily Layer 4, with Layer 3 as secondary. Layers 1–2 treated as context.

**Characteristic items:** CIA Framework; Authority Shifts from Office to Personality Platforms; Algorithmic Opacity Challenges Legibility Principle; Tech Feudal Kingmaker Model.

### Middle Corpus (Jan–Jun 2025): Political Crisis & Resilience Focus

**Dominant themes:** State capture mechanisms, erosion of civil service independence, mutual aid and distributed resistance, financial system legitimacy breakdown, care infrastructure.

**Layer shift:** Layer 4 remains dominant but Layers 1, 2, and 6 gain prominence — shift from analyzing authority capture to building resilient alternatives.

**Characteristic items:** Trump Regime Begins: Not Populist; Farm Labor, Prison Labor, Food Systems; Mutual Aid in LA; Metabolic Rift and Los Angeles; Great Trump Depression; The Father Figure Is a Lie.

### Recent Corpus (Jul 2025–Mar 2026): Infrastructure & Governance Innovation

**Dominant themes:** Food systems and agricultural transformation, local resistance and municipal governance, epistemic frameworks, indigenous sovereignty, information warfare, care-based models.

**Layer shift:** Layers 1, 2, and 6 now equal or exceed Layer 4. Layer 5 gains emphasis. Authority critique deprioritized; alternative construction foregrounded.

**Characteristic items:** Hidden Architecture of Food; The Soft Annexation of the Prairies; First Nations and Next Generation Farmers; From Taxpayers to Caretakers; Notes from the Epistemic Workshop; The Ecology of Intelligence.

### Overall Pattern

Clear three-stage evolution:
1. **Crisis Detection** (Oct–Dec 2024): Identify authority threats
2. **Institutional Failure** (Jan–Jun 2025): Demonstrate inadequacy of existing systems
3. **Alternative Construction** (Jul 2025–Mar 2026): Articulate and analyze emerging governance models

## 8. KEY OBSERVATIONS

### Taxonomy Gaps

1. **Private/Transnational Authority** — Current Stack assumes state-centric authority. Tech billionaires, corporate platforms, and private military contractors wield state-equivalent coercive power without formal state status. May warrant a new layer or significant Layer 4 extension.

2. **Linguistic/Semantic Colonization** — Language replacement by technical/corporate terms operates at a meta-layer affecting both Layer 4 and Layer 5 but isn't fully captured by either.

3. **Cross-layer Interdependencies** — Material base directly constrains epistemic output. Current framework treats Layers 1 and 5 as independent when the corpus shows them tightly coupled.

4. **Care-Based Governance** — Layer 6 is underrepresented (6%) despite growing corpus emphasis. The care-vs.-coercion frame may deserve elevation from a layer to a cross-cutting principle.

5. **Governance Resistance Tactics** — Constituent pressure, ombudsman strategies, public naming are meta-governance tactics distinct from authority patterns and from Stack design proposals.

### Programmatic Arc Hypothesis

The corpus reflects a deliberate arc: Crisis Detection → Institutional Failure → Alternative Construction. This suggests the Autonomy Stack is functioning as a roadmap for governance transformation, not merely documentation of current power structures. The Stack's next phase of development should reflect this — moving from diagnostic architecture toward design and operationalization.
