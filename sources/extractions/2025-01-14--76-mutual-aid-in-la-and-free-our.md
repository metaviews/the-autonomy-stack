### SIGNAL
Crisis Events Expose Centralized Institutional Weaknesses
**Synthesized Content**
Institutional authority faces legitimacy erosion during acute disasters, prompting communities to bypass official structures. This shift creates space for alternative governance models rooted in direct action rather than hierarchy. The phenomenon is visible when formal response mechanisms fail to meet immediate material or informational needs.

**Source Note**
Paraphrased from newsletter opening section on LA wildfires and subsequent observation of grassroots replacement of government agencies during Eaton Fire events.

### PATTERN CANDIDATE
Informal Networks Scale Faster Than Bureaucracies
**Synthesized Content**
The phenomenon involves grassroots actors mobilizing resources and information more rapidly than state-sanctioned institutions during emergencies. This occurs because informal groups leverage local knowledge and decentralized communication tools without requiring bureaucratic approvals. The consequence is a temporary power shift where community networks hold more efficacy than official governance bodies, highlighting a systemic resilience gap.

**Source Note**
Synthesized from observations of Mutual Aid LA Network, Santa Anita Park hubs, and Altadena Girls Recovery Fund outperforming institutional speed during the LA fires.

This extends the Commons layer by treating informal networks as critical infrastructure. It complicates current framing by suggesting material survival relies on reciprocal reciprocity rather than public agency supply chains.

If authority is defined by care, it risks becoming moralized and difficult to challenge without appearing unkind, potentially freezing power dynamics under a veil of benevolence rather than maintaining open contestation.
