### CONCEPT

**Algorithmic Sanctions Evasion**
**Definition:** The use of social media algorithms and direct-to-consumer digital channels to bypass state-enforced trade barriers or sanctions without physical confrontation.
**Connection:** Relates to Layer 3 (Legibility/Metrics) and Layer 4 (Decision Systems). Suggests that if algorithms control distribution, state metrics of trade become obsolete.

**Corporate Policy Abolitionism**
**Definition:** A strategy where tech elites leverage public platforms to urge the dissolution of existing regulatory frameworks, framing deregulation as innovation or liberation.
**Connection:** Connects to Layer 4 (Decision Systems) and the concept of "Authority." Questions if "expertise" is being replaced by "influence" in governance.
**Source:** Dorsey's call to "delete all IP law" and the subsequent narrative of unleashing AI innovation.

**Digital Hegemony Infrastructure**
**Definition:** The assumption that platform ecosystems (like TikTok) function as sovereign territories capable of executing state-like functions (trade, propaganda, surveillance) within national borders.
**Connection:** Challenges Layer 3 and Layer 1. Suggests platforms have "Material" power (infrastructure access) alongside "Content" power.

### PROVOCATION

**Legibility vs. Safety Paradox**
The newsletter suggests that the increased "legibility" of trade goods via TikTok obscures the dangerous lack of visibility regarding surveillance and security infrastructure. This challenges the Stack's principle of "Legibility over expertise." If legible information hides lethal risks, is legibility the right goal, or is it a vulnerability?

**IP Abolition as Class Neutral Illusion**
The argument that abolishing IP law benefits "ordinary creators" is presented alongside evidence it serves "entrenched corporate giants." This complicates Stack Principle: "Contestable Authority." If authority (law) is removed to help "creators," but power concentrates in the "tech giants," who actually holds the authority to contest?
