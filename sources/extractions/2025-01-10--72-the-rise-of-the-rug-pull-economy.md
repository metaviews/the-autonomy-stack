### SIGNAL
**Policy Shift Toward Wealth Creation Over Protection**
The incoming government administration signals explicit prioritization of economic growth and innovation in the cryptocurrency sector over regulatory safeguards or investor protection. This marks a structural shift where unchecked market activity is framed as a cornerstone of national economic strategy rather than a risk requiring governance.

*Source note:* Paraphrase of the section discussing the incoming administration’s focus on wealth creation and digital assets as engines of growth without addressing fraud gaps.

### PATTERN CANDIDATE
**The High-Velocity Hype-Exploit Cycle**
*   **Phenomenon:** Scammers leverage rapid social media amplification and speculative fervor to inflate asset values, then disappear with funds before accountability mechanisms can engage.
*   **Context:** Decentralized finance (DeFi) and digital asset markets characterized by low barriers to entry and high volatility.
*   **Forces:** Anonymity, lack of regulatory oversight, speed of information spread, and incentive structures favoring immediate cash-out over long-term viability.
*   **Consequences:** Systemic entrenchment of wealth inequality, erosion of trust in markets, and normalization of predatory financial behavior. If ignored, this pattern validates exploitation as a viable governance model.

*Cross-reference: PATTERN CANDIDATE: See CONCEPT: "Rug Pull Economy" for systemic framing.*

### STACK MATERIAL
**Layer 3 (Legibility & Metrics): Legibility Gap in Decentralized Finance**
Current market metrics prioritize speed and valuation growth over real utility, making systems effectively "blind" to fraud until after damage occurs. The text highlights that anonymity is used as a shield against accountability, directly contradicting the Stack principle that power without legibility is unacceptable. This challenges the ability of any system to respond to harm.

**Layer 4 (Decision Systems & Authority): Administrative Prioritization of Growth**
Decision-making authority is exercised to curtail regulation rather than protect participants. This creates a governance void where the state incentivizes the conditions necessary for fraud (speculative excess) to occur. The current framing treats fraud as an externality rather than a failure of the administrative decision system itself.

**Layer 6 (Human Capacity & Care): Neglect of Non-Material Consequences**
Financial loss results in severe human tolls including bankruptcy and suicide, yet the current system treats these solely as market corrections rather than care failures. The Stack’s focus on human capacity requires accounting for psychological devastation and disability inclusion (financial exclusion) as part of the material base.

*Cross-reference: See PATTERN CANDIDATE: High-Velocity Hype-Exploit Cycle for the mechanism of loss.*

### CONCEPT
**Rug Pull Economy**
*Definition:* Beyond a specific act of fraud, a systemic economic model where wealth redistribution is driven by the normalization of extraction, speculative manipulation, and the absence of redress mechanisms. In this economy, the most effective strategy for accumulation is the exploitation of information asymmetry and trust.
*Connection:* Extends the Stack’s understanding of economic systems in Layer 1 (Material Base) to include information architecture and trust as material resources.

**Speculative Excess**
*Definition:* A cultural and market condition where the primary motivation for participation is rapid valuation growth rather than utility, creating incentives for deception. This condition overrides innovation and prioritizes gambling over construction.
*Connection:* Relates to Layer 5 (Knowledge & Intelligence) regarding shared situational awareness and the narratives required to sustain market behavior.

**Authoritative Neutrality**
*Definition:* The stance where governance actors frame themselves as non-interventionist in the name of innovation or freedom, effectively permitting harm to occur without liability.
*Connection:* Challenges Stack Principle: "Authority must be contestable." If authority remains neutral in the face of theft, contestability becomes impossible.

### PROVOCATION
**Irreversibility vs. "Nothing Is Final"**
The Stack principle states "nothing is final," yet crypto rug pulls introduce a technical and legal finality to financial theft that traditional governance struggles to remedy. If financial transactions become irreversible by design, does the Stack's view of fallible, revisable systems apply? We must determine if "irreversibility" is a design feature or a systemic failure condition for governance.
