### SIGNAL

**Authority Shifting to Behavioral Sorting**
Clinical diagnosis is being bypassed by data infrastructure that profiles neurodivergence through behavioral patterns (language, engagement, eye movement) rather than medical need. This transforms difference from a health condition into a risk signal for algorithmic sorting, prioritizing categorization over care.
*Source Note: Newsletter argues identification is happening via data infrastructure rather than doctors, profiling based on behavior patterns.*
*Cross-Reference: Also logged as PATTERN CANDIDATE in Decision Systems.*

### PATTERN CANDIDATE

**Algorithmic Gatekeeping of Social Participation**
*Phenomenon:* Automated systems exclude individuals based on inferred behavioral traits rather than direct interaction.
*Context:* Hiring platforms, EdTech software, immigration screening, and public benefits.
*Forces:* Efficiency metrics, risk aversion, and the demand for scalable compliance monitoring.
*Consequences:* Normalization of exclusion based on statistical deviation; reduction of dissent to technical error; removal of due process.
*Source Note: Observes hiring, EdTech, and immigration systems flagging "risk" or "fit" based on engagement patterns rather than clinical need.*

### CONCEPT

**Algorithmic Gaze: Data-First Surveillance**
A governance surveillance mechanism where systemic power interprets user behavior not as communication but as sortable data patterns. It functions by inferring internal states (neurology, stability) from external metrics without consent, enabling sorting based on statistical deviation rather than documented action.
*Source Note: Defines AI categorization as "inference" rather than "understanding" or "diagnosis."*
*Cross-Reference: Core to Stack Layer 3 (Legibility).*

**Preemptive Classification: Risk Without Claim**
A decision authority model where status, safety, or eligibility is assigned to individuals prior to any direct interaction or claim. It bypasses standard administrative review by using predictive data to assign risk profiles, effectively granting machines the power to gatekeep participation before humans do.
*Source Note: Describes systems that flag "at-risk" students or "fit" candidates based on engagement.*
*Cross-Reference: Core to Stack Layer 4 (Decision Systems).*
