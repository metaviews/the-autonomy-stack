### PATTERN CANDIDATE
**Title:** Retreat From Institutional Constraints To Unilateral Authority Dynamics
**Phenomenon:** Established governance structures (international law, UN, alliances) are increasingly ignored or bypassed when they conflict with immediate strategic interests of major states.
**Context:** Great power interactions involving territorial claims and humanitarian crises where institutional mediation fails or is discarded.
**Forces:** Political preference for disruptive gestures over diplomacy; erosion of trust in public agencies; rise of unilateral nationalism.
**Consequences:** Global order becomes less predictable and more violent; smaller nations lose protective frameworks; "might makes right" becomes operational doctrine.
**Source Note:** Analysis of the rejection of the Gaza plan by regional and international bodies, yet noting the U.S. administration disregards diplomatic norms, indicating a broader trend beyond one event.

### CONCEPT
**Title:** Post-WWII Order As Governance Architecture
**Definition:** A governance framework where state authority is constrained by multilateral institutions, international treaties, and human rights norms rather than unilateral capability.
**Connections:** Connects to Layer 4 (Decision Systems) and Layer 2 (Commons & Institutions). Represents the "rules of the game" the Stack relies upon for stability.
**Source Note:** Described as the framework built on the idea that atrocities like forced displacement should never happen again, currently under threat.
**Cross-reference:** Relates to the erosion noted in PATTERN CANDIDATE.

**Title:** Power Legitimacy Versus Legal Legitimacy
**Definition:** "Power Legitimacy" derives authority from capability to enforce; "Legal Legitimacy" derives authority from adherence to codified procedure.
**Connections:** Layer 4 (Decision Systems). The tension between who *can* act versus who is *allowed* to act.
**Source Note:** Contrasted in the text through the rejection of the proposal by international bodies versus the U.S. administration's disregard for norms.
**Cross-reference:** Provocative tension with core Stack Principle: "Authority must be contestable."

### PROVOCATION
**Title:** Contestability Fails When Power Dictates Legitimacy
**Challenge:** The Stack assumes authority is contestable. If "might makes right" becomes the operational standard, the contestability mechanism breaks down. There is no procedural lever to push back against force.
**Complication:** If power without legal legibility is acceptable, the Stack's core defense ("power without legibility is unacceptable") becomes a preference, not a constraint. Does resilience rely on rules that are no longer universally enforced?
**Source Note:** Derived from the assertion that the world is witnessing the normalization of conquest and a return to a model where power alone dictates legitimacy.
