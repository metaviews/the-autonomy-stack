### SIGNAL

**Fluency Masks Competence in Governance**
Leaders increasingly equate AI’s smooth output with actual understanding, projecting decisiveness while lacking technical grasp of the systems they command. This tension between institutional confidence and epistemic void creates fragile authority structures reliant on illusions of control.

Leaders project control over probabilistic systems (military, safety, education) to satisfy expectations of command, ignoring the mismatch between legal authority and technical mastery. This dynamic is driven by the prestige of office laundering misunderstanding, resulting in procurement based on assumption rather than empirical stability.

### CONCEPT

**Probabilistic Literacy**
A foundational understanding that AI outputs are statistical approximations rather than deterministic truths. Includes fluency in core properties: alignment fragility, structural hallucination, and adversarial manipulation. It is not coding knowledge but epistemic safety training for leaders.
*Connection:* Relates to Legibility & Metrics (measurement of uncertainty) and Decision Systems (criteria for command).

**Epistemic Shallowness**
A state of leadership where decision-making relies on confident assertions that contradict the actual operational understanding of the tools employed. It occurs when authority masks technical illiteracy with institutional prestige, rendering governance theatrical rather than functional.
*Connection:* Relates to Decision Systems & Authority and Human Capacity (competence requirements).

**Alignment as Statistical Pressure**
The mechanism of safety constraints in AI models functions as distributed statistical pressure rather than discrete switch-based controls. Adjusting constraints in one domain reshapes behavior in others, contradicting the modular tool model.
*Connection:* Relates to Material Base (logistics of control) and Decision Systems (safeguards design).

### PROVOCATION

**Legibility Fails Without Probabilistic Literacy**
The Stack principle "legibility over expertise" risks becoming theater if the metrics themselves are statistical approximations. Understanding the system's opacity may require specific expertise, challenging the notion that shared legibility is sufficient for contestable authority.
*Connection:* Challenges Core Principle (Legibility over Expertise).
*Implication:* Resilience requires acknowledging opacity, not just making systems visible.
