### CONCEPT

**FAFO (Fuck Around Find Out) as Governance Feedback**
*Proposed Definition:* A descriptor for the moment when strategic or ideological errors become materially tangible through system failure, fiscal crisis, or social unrest. It marks the transition where abstraction meets consequence, often serving as a late-stage warning signal that is ignored until the "payback" phase.
*Connection:* Extends Stack Principle "Failure is informative." Unlike standard feedback, FAFO implies a punitive or inevitable consequence rather than a corrective learning opportunity without systemic change.

**InfoSec Common Sense as Institutional Competence Indicator**
*Proposed Definition:* The operational discipline required to maintain security tools effectively; lacking this, secrecy tools become liabilities that expose authority to public scrutiny. Competence in information security is not just technical but political, as it determines what the public can see or know about state decisions.
*Connection:* Connects to Stack Layer 3 (Legibility & Metrics) regarding what systems can see and measure, and Layer 6 (Human Capacity & Care) regarding the training required for authority figures.

### PROVOCATION

**Digital Performance Undermines Structural Authority Legitimacy**
The line between power and performance is razor thin in the digital age. When authority relies on the performance of secrecy (e.g., secure chats) rather than robust institutional processes, accidental exposure (leaks) renders that authority illegitimate regardless of strategic intent. This suggests that legibility may be unavoidable even for those seeking obscurity, challenging the Stack's "authority contestable" principle by suggesting that *accidental* contestability is now the baseline risk.

*Source Note:* Derived from the analysis of the Signal breach and the observation that tools of secrecy amplify risk when used recklessly.
