# We Should Hire More Social Workers

**Instead of policing let's focus on care**  
*2024-12-07*

---

### SIGNAL

Title: Cities Shift Security Focus From Control To Care

Synthesized content: Municipal strategies for managing public space crises are shifting from containment-oriented security to support-oriented social work. This transition reframes homelessness and mental health incidents not as property threats but as health needs requiring intervention. The underlying assumption is that proactive care yields better community outcomes than reactive enforcement.

Source note: The newsletter argues that hiring social workers instead of security guards in public spaces addresses the root causes of crisis and reduces downstream costs associated with ignoring social issues.

Cross-reference: Also logged as PATTERN CANDIDATE: Care-Based Deployment Replaces Enforcement In Public Spaces.

### PATTERN CANDIDATE

Title: Care-Based Deployment Replaces Enforcement In Public Spaces

Synthesized content: This pattern describes the substitution of coercive security roles with supportive care roles within contested public zones.
Phenomenon: Security guards at doorways/parks replaced by social workers.
Context: High inequality, housing crises, and mental health needs intersecting with public infrastructure.
Forces: Rising costs of enforcement versus long-term social stability; cultural desire for humane management of crisis.
Consequences: Engagement shifts from suppression to resource navigation. Ignoring this pattern maintains systems that displace vulnerable populations rather than integrate them.

Source note: The text outlines a vision where social workers are paired with specialists to form collaborative teams funded by municipalities and nonprofits to address crises from multiple angles.

Cross-reference: Also logged as STACK MATERIAL: Care Labor Prioritizes Root Cause Prevention.

### STACK MATERIAL

Title: Authority Rooted In Care Rather Than Command

Relevant Layer: 4 (Decision Systems & Authority)
Synthesized content: Authority is reconceptualized not as the power to enforce rules, but as the capacity to navigate complex systems and build trust. Social workers exercise legitimacy through action and resource connection rather than command, challenging the definition of governance authority as solely coercive.
Source note: The newsletter posits that there is an authority rooted in care and mutual aid which earns trust through action rather than command.
Cross-reference: Also logged as CONCEPT: Care Authority Defined By Support Trust Mechanism.

Title: Care Workers Generate Systemic Barrier Data

Relevant Layer: 3 (Legibility & Metrics)
Synthesized content: Frontline care providers function as a data source, identifying systemic barriers that standard metrics miss. Their daily interactions with vulnerable populations create feedback loops on what policies and resources fail to cover. This establishes legibility from the ground up rather than top-down administration.
Source note: The text suggests social workers can serve as a critical source of data on the systemic barriers people face.
Cross-reference: Also logged as STACK MATERIAL: Care Workers Generate Systemic Barrier Data (Self-reference in Layer context).

Title: Care Labor Prioritizes Root Cause Prevention

Relevant Layer: 6 (Human Capacity & Care)
Synthesized content: Security roles are reactive (calling services after a problem occurs), whereas social work roles are proactive (preventing crises). This redefines the labor function of public service from reaction to intervention, shifting human capacity toward long-term stability and health.
Source note: The text highlights that social workers are proactive, preventing small crises from becoming large ones compared to reactive security guards and police.

### CONCEPT

Title: Care Authority Defined By Support Trust Mechanism

Synthesized content: A framework distinguishing between Enforcement Authority (power to punish) and Care Authority (power to connect). Car Authority derives legitimacy from the ability to solve the user’s problem through resource access and stability, rather than compliance with norms.
Definition: Legitimacy earned through successful outcomes in navigating systems and providing support, rather than compliance via coercion.
Connection: Expands Layer 4 Decision Systems & Authority. Challenges the Stack's default association of authority with law and administrative processes by including mutual aid models as authority structures.
Source note: The newsletter introduces the idea that authority can be rooted in care and mutual aid, earned through action rather than command.

### PROVOCATION

Title: Care Programs Risk Expanding Surveillance Capacity

Synthesized content: Increasing the footprint of state-connected care in public spaces may expand bureaucratic visibility over vulnerable populations, even if intent is benevolent. If "care" becomes a mandatory deployment of social workers in public spaces, does consent diminish? This challenges the principle that authority must be contestable, as care roles may lack clear exit mechanisms for recipients.
Source note: Implicit tension in the text's call for more social workers without discussing the mechanics of consensual engagement or refusal.
