### CONCEPT

**Public Health Analogy Applied to Cyber Risk**
A framework for governance proposing that digital threats should be treated like public health diseases—confronted directly to maintain community well-being rather than avoided due to stigma.
Source Note: "Moving Beyond Taboo" section comparing dark web engagement to handling disease.

**State and Local Cybersecurity Grant Program (SLCGP)**
A specific federal funding mechanism enabling states and localities to purchase digital risk solutions, including dark web monitoring, to protect infrastructure and constituents.
Source Note: Resource list section detailing SLCGP utility for municipalities.

**Institutional Digital Risk Programs**
Formal organizational structures within government to continuously monitor, assess, and mitigate risks to digital infrastructure, funded via specific grant mechanisms rather than ad-hoc police action.
Source Note: SLCGP resources and discussion of organizational readiness questions.

### PROVOCATION

**Claiming Technology Neutrality Masks Embedded Power Structures**
The assertion that the dark web is a neutral tool shaped only by use obscures the inherent political structure of anonymity that protects specific power dynamics from accountability.
Source Note: Argument in "Moving Beyond Taboo" that the dark web is a neutral technology.

**Policing Monopoly Undermines Public Authority Contestability**
Assigning exclusive responsibility for digital safety to law enforcement creates an authority structure that the community cannot contest or fully understand, risking unchecked power.
