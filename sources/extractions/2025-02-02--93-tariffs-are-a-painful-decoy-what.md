# SIGNAL
### Tariffs as a Strategic Distraction Tactic
Tariff policies function less as economic regulation and more as deliberate mechanisms to induce economic shock, distracting public attention while structural power shifts occur elsewhere. This decouples policy intent from stated goals, using crisis as cover for executive consolidation. The immediate economic pain (auto industry, energy prices) is treated as collateral damage rather than a policy failure.
**Source Note:** The newsletter argues that the contradictory nature of tariffs on Canada and Mexico is intentional, designed to overwhelm the public and media while the state is reconfigured undemocratically.

# PATTERN CANDIDATE
### Crisis Engineering for Asset and Authority Consolidation
**Phenomenon:** Political actors leverage economic instability or trade disputes to destabilize existing industries or government functions.
**Context:** High-frequency trade wars, defense sector shifts, and digital platform centralization.
**Forces:** Concentrated capital (billionaire oligarchies) seeking deregulation and direct market control; executive desire to bypass legislative check; technology enabling rapid systemic intervention (AI/automation).
**Consequences:** Privatization of essential functions, reduced human oversight in critical infrastructure, and erosion of legislative power. If ignored, this pattern normalizes the bypassing of democratic consensus in favor of "disruption" metrics.
**Also logged as STACK MATERIAL: Executive Encroachment on Legislative Funding Power**, **CONCEPT: Move Fast and Break Things at Scale**.
