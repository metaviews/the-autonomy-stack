### PATTERN CANDIDATE

**Legal Dualism Splits Authority Between Normative And Arbitrary**
Phenomenon: Society fractures into two legal regimes—one based on logic and rights, the other on arbitrary prerogative. Context: Emerges during rapid authoritarian rise involving divided populations. Forces: Political polarization and security state expansion. Consequences: Erosion of universal rights creates a subject class where law enforcement is a threat rather than protection; legitimacy becomes dependent on fear. Ignoring this dynamic normalizes the erosion of rights for specific groups.
Source note: Text describes the "dual state" where "law is logical, one where the law is arbitrary and arguably nonexistent" for certain groups.
Cross-reference: Also logged as CONCEPT: Dual State and STACK MATERIAL: Layer 4 Authority Bifurcation.

### CONCEPT

**Dual State: Coexistence Of Normative And Prerogative Law**
A framework where two distinct legal orders exist simultaneously: one normative (logical, procedural) and one prerogative (arbitrary, subjective). This divides the populace into those protected by rights and those subject to rule. Connects to Stack Principle: "Authority must be contestable," as this system removes contestability for the "latter group."
Source note: Description of "two systems of law" in the text distinguishing between logical and arbitrary law application.

**Ineffective Police State: Relies On Symbolism Over Enforcement**
A governance model that maintains order through visible presence and fear tactics despite lacking the actual capacity to enforce policy or govern effectively. It proceeds via "stumbles and spectacle" rather than functional administration. Connects to Principle: "Resilience over elegance," as this state is rigid but brittle.
Source note: Analysis of the police state's reliance on "symbolism, fear, and distraction" due to lack of effective enforcement capacity.

**Frog Syndrome: Gradual Normalization Of Surveillance Crisis**
A metaphor derived from the "boiling frog" theory, applied to information and political reality. Citizens are "data points in a surveillance society" failing to recognize incremental escalation of authoritarianism until the "water is getting warmer." Connects to Principle: "Nothing is final," warning against static perception of past safety.
Source note: Metaphor used to describe the rapid, deceptive rise of authoritarianism and the difficulty in recognizing the crisis amidst the "water."
