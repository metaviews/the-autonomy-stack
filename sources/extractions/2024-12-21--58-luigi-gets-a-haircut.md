## PATTERN CANDIDATE

**State Escalates Charges to Suppress Dissent Spread**
*   **Phenomenon:** The legal system applies maximum severity charges (e.g., terrorism) not merely to adjudicate the specific crime, but to control the broader symbolic implications of the act.
*   **Context:** Appears when individual violence targets high-profile institutions during periods of high public grievance (e.g., economic inequity, healthcare access).
*   **Forces:** Produced by the state’s need to maintain stability and legitimacy when faced with narratives that frame violence as a symptom of systemic failure.
*   **Consequences:** Engaging in this pattern risks amplifying the dissident’s message rather than deterring it, as the charge itself validates the narrative of state overreach; ignoring it may embolden future symbolic attacks.

## PROVOCATION

**Deterrent Punishment Undermines Authority Legitimacy Long-term**
This case suggests that maximizing punitive measures to secure order may directly erode the authority being protected. If the law is perceived primarily as a tool for silencing systemic critique rather than resolving harm, it may lose its function as a stable social contract. The state attempts to reassert control through severity but risks confirming the narrative that authority is purely coercive, thereby reducing long-term resilience.
