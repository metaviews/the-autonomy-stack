**Title:** Algorithmic Moderation Blind Spot
**Synthesized Content:** A structural defect in governance systems designed to detect prohibited vocabulary (explicit hate) while failing to flag prohibited *intent* conveyed through context or proper nouns. It enables dog whistles to flourish because they occupy the gap between semantic legibility and metric visibility.

### PROVOCATION

**Title:** Legibility of Harm vs. Legibility of Law
**Synthesized Content:** The Stack prioritizes "legibility over expertise" and demands power be legible to contest. However, this issue demonstrates that "shared comprehension" of a slur is highly legible (the audience understands perfectly) yet actively undermines authority contestability. If a slur creates a silent consensus that is mutually understood (legible) but explicitly unspoken, does it satisfy the requirement for contestable authority? Or does "Power without Legibility" also apply to unspoken consensus that machines can't see? This suggests "Legibility" may need to be redefined to include *contextual intent detection*, not just lexical visibility.
