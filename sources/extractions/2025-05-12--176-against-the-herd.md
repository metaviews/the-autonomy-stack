# 176: Against the Herd

**Gaza, Immigration, and the War on Mutual Aid Disguised as Law**  
*2025-05-12*

---

### SIGNAL

**Law Inverts into Domination Against Solidarity**
The newsletter observes a systemic shift where legal frameworks are actively repurposed to dismantle collective agency rather than protect it. This inversion turns solidarity into a subversive act, particularly evident in geopolitical zones like Gaza and migration corridors.

Newsletters argue that in Gaza and at borders, legality is claimed by violators while law becomes a weapon to fragment communities instead of restraining power.

*Also logged as PATTERN CANDIDATE: State-Engineered Fragmentation to Suppress Collective Agency.*

### PATTERN CANDIDATE

**State-Engineered Fragmentation to Suppress Collective Agency**
A recurring dynamic where rulers criminalize migration and mutual aid to prevent coordinated survival. By targeting the social fabric required for collective defense, the state ensures dependency on hierarchy and prevents emergent leadership from forming within vulnerable populations.

Text describes how states seek to scatter and isolate Palestinians and migrants, making their movement a crime to break bonds of care.

*Also logged as STACK MATERIAL: Mutual Aid as Distributed Leadership Protocol.*

### STACK MATERIAL

**Mutual Aid as Distributed Leadership Protocol**
Herds function through real-time negotiation and consensus without hierarchy. This maps to a governance layer where authority is not held by a leader but distributed across the network, requiring continuous relational maintenance rather than static rules.

Author observes goats making decisions by consensus, reading each other, with every individual capable of leading while backed by the group.

**Relevant Layers:** Layer 2 (Commons & Institutions), Layer 6 (Human Capacity & Care).

*Also logged as CONCEPT: Mutual Aid as Kropotkinian Survival Principle.*

### STACK MATERIAL

**Relational Law Supersedes Statutory Command**
Real law is described as born from messy, negotiated spaces of relationships, contrasting with edicts from above. This challenges the Authority layer by suggesting legitimacy stems from shared risk and care rather than administrative decree.

Newsletter cites Graeber to argue real law comes from relationships and democratic spaces where people figure out how to live and fight.

**Relevant Layers:** Layer 4 (Decision Systems & Authority).

### CONCEPT

**Mutual Aid as Kropotkinian Survival Principle**
A framework identifying mutual care and resource sharing as the biological and social basis of survival, predating the state. It suggests that authority emerges from voluntary coordination and interdependence rather than imposed command structures.

Text references Peter Kropotkin to describe the law of mutuality found in animal herds and human communities as a sophisticated form of governance lacking hierarchy.

*Also logged as STACK MATERIAL: Mutual Aid as Distributed Leadership Protocol.*

### CONCEPT

**Inversion of Law into Domination**
A mechanism where violence becomes the law itself, neutralizing legal protections to enforce political control. It reframes legality not as protection but as a tool of the powerful to criminalize solidarity.

Text references David Graeber’s argument that international law is undermined when war crimes occur with impunity under the language of legality.

*Also logged as PROVOCATION: Law Serves Power Not Community Protection.*

### CONCEPT

**State Origin Through Destruction of Commons**
A historical lens arguing that modern states emerged by destroying the commons, breaking bonds of mutual care, and criminalizing solidarity to establish monopoly on force.

Newsletter cites Silvia Federici to argue that the current war on solidarity is a continuation of the original process where the state destroys pre-existing community structures.

### CONCEPT

**Grievable Life Bias in Legal Framework**
A lens identifying how systems of justice protect only certain populations by defining which lives are worthy of mourning and legal recourse. It explains why some herds receive protection while others face siege.

Newsletter cites Judith Butler to note that rights and safety reflect bias in which lives are seen as grievable versus expendable.

### PROVOCATION

**Law Serves Power Not Community Protection**
Challenges the Stack’s assumption that systems can be made legible to ensure accountability. If law is structurally weaponized against the vulnerable, legibility may simply expose the mechanisms of domination rather than resolve them.

Text asserts that under the current global order, law exists primarily to discipline, scatter, and dominate the herd rather than protect it.

*Also logged as CONCEPT: Inversion of Law into Domination.*
