### STACK MATERIAL

**Title 1: Global IP Frameworks as Enforcement Mechanisms** [Layer 4: Decision Systems]
**Content:** International legal bodies like WTO and WIPO function primarily as enforcement mechanisms for US economic hegemony rather than neutral governance. Compliance with IP regimes acts as a prerequisite for market access, making legal frameworks a primary site of power struggle where sovereignty conflicts with corporate rights. Disagreement over these rules is treated as legal violation rather than policy debate.
**Source Note:** Refers to how US enforces global IP laws through trade deals and international bodies to control markets and extract value.
**Cross-reference:** Also logged as PATTERN CANDIDATE: Tech Sovereignty.

**Title 2: Seed Patents as Agricultural Control Infrastructure** [Layer 1: Material Base]
**Content:** Industrial agriculture relies on closed ecosystems of patented seeds, proprietary software, and sensor networks that trap farmers. Removing these legal barriers enables local manufacturing of open-source farm tech and climate-resistant seeds, directly impacting food sovereignty and resilience. Current structures prioritize extraction and maintenance of monopolies over environmental adaptation or farmer autonomy.
**Source Note:** Discussion of agriculture sector breakdown under IP monopoly and potential open model enabling food sovereignty.

**Title 3: Criminalization of Creative Remix and Access** [Layer 6: Human Capacity]
**Content:** Current IP systems criminalize remixing and limit access to tools essential for cultural production. Decriminalizing creativity through copyright reform would shift value from ownership rights to usage rights, enhancing human capacity for innovation without corporate permission. This reframes the creator not as a rights-holder but as a node in a shared knowledge network.
**Source Note:** Canada section suggestion on decriminalizing creativity and protecting cultural remix and public knowledge.
**Cross-reference:** Also logged as CONCEPT: Decriminalization of Creativity.

### CONCEPT

**Title 1: Intellectual Property as Imperial Infrastructure**
**Content:** A framework defining IP not merely as legal protection for invention, but as systemic infrastructure used to maintain geopolitical dominance and extract value. It treats ownership of ideas as a lever for controlling economic sovereignty in subordinate regions, functioning like pipelines or trade routes for power distribution.
**Source Note:** Direct conceptualization from text section defining US enforcement of IP laws as global infrastructure.
**Cross-reference:** Connects to Decision Systems layer.

**Title 2: Reverse Marshall Plan**
**Content:** A strategy where a state projects power not through financial aid alone, but by providing open-source tools and liberated knowledge systems. Aims to establish alliances and influence in the Global South by reducing extractive relationships, offering technological sovereignty rather than debt dependency.
**Source Note:** Comparison of China open innovation pivot to historical Marshall Plan, noting difference in tools vs cash.
**Cross-reference:** Connects to Commons & Institutions layer.

**Title 3: Open Innovation as Post-Trade System**
**Content:** A socio-technical arrangement where knowledge flows freely without licensing restrictions or IP enforcement. Functions as a survival mechanism in fragmented markets, prioritizing collaboration over restriction to achieve national sovereignty and global accessibility. Contradicts traditional trade theory by making "access" the currency rather than "exclusion."
**Source Note:** Text definition of China pivot from IP recognition to sharing open technologies as a strategy.
**Cross-reference:** Also logged as PATTERN CANDIDATE.
