# Data Isn't the New Oil—It's Just Noise

**Attention Is The Real Scarce Resource**  
*2024-11-27*

---

### SIGNAL

**Shift from Data Scarcity to Attention Scarcity Rhetoric**
Governance discourse regarding digital infrastructure is pivoting from treating data volume as a primary asset to recognizing human attention as the actual constraint on value creation.
Source: Newsletter argues the "data is oil" metaphor obscures the reality that information abundance creates attention poverty.

### PATTERN CANDIDATE

**Resource Obfuscation: Masking Extraction Beneath Abundance Claims**
Phenomenon: Systems claim authority over abundant, non-scarce inputs (data) to legitimize the extraction of scarce human outputs (attention, time).
Context: Occurs in platform governance, AI deployment, and digital advertising policy.
Forces: Economic incentives to valorize existing infrastructure assets while externalizing human cognitive costs; AI automation reducing human labor value.
Consequences: Regulatory focus misdirected toward data privacy rather than attention rights, cognitive sovereignty, and surveillance capitalism.
Cross-reference: Also logged as STACK MATERIAL under Legibility & Metrics (Layer 3) and Human Capacity & Care (Layer 6).

### STACK MATERIAL

**Attention Scarcity as a Constraint on Layer 6 Systems**
Relevant Layer: Human Capacity & Care (Layer 6)
Analysis: Governance must recognize attention as a finite biological resource rather than renewable. Current systems treat it as infinite, depleting the human base layer through algorithmic surveillance to drive action.
Source: Newsletter posits attention as finite, intrinsically valuable resource being monopolized by algorithms, contrasting with abundant data.

**Metrics of Legibility Must Prioritize Cognitive Impact**
Relevant Layer: Legibility & Metrics (Layer 3)
Analysis: Current metrics track data accumulation (legibility of volume). Effective governance requires measuring cognitive capture and manipulation (legibility of harm) rather than just information flow.
Source: Analysis notes current rhetoric conflates data utility with intrinsic value despite data being "noise" without processing.

**Labor Obsolescence in Data Value Chain Automation**
Relevant Layer: Decision Systems & Authority (Layer 4) / Human Capacity & Care (Layer 6)
Analysis: The rise of AI automates traditional data handling roles, shifting the locus of power from the workers managing data to the owners of the attention-grabbing algorithms.
Source: Newsletter notes data handling roles being made obsolete by AI emergence, reducing data processing value.

### CONCEPT

**Herbert Simon's Attention Economy Principle**
Proposed Definition: A framework asserting that information abundance creates a corresponding deficiency in the attention required to process it, shifting value to the interface managing scarcity.
Connection: Informs Layer 6 (Human Capacity) regarding the limits of cognitive authority.
Source: Newsletter references Simon on information wealth creating attention poverty.

**Tim Wu's Attention Merchants Model**
Proposed Definition: Describes economic systems where data is merely a tool to capture and exploit human attention for profit, rather than a standalone commodity.
Connection: Defines power dynamics in Layer 4 (Decision Systems), where authority is derived from attention monopolies.
Source: Newsletter cites Tim Wu to illustrate data utilization for profit extraction via attention capture.

**The Data Is New Oil Metaphor Flaw**
Proposed Definition: An analytical critique identifying the "data as oil" analogy as a flawed governance model that treats an infinite resource as finite/valuable to mask attention extraction.
Connection: Directly challenges assumptions in Layer 3 (Legibility) regarding the value of metrics and data collection.
Source: Newsletter argues the metaphor is garbage that distracts from the true source of value.

### PROVOCATION

**Legibility Over Expertise May Ignore Cognitive Sovereignty**
Challenge to Principle: The Stack principle "legibility over expertise" assumes transparency of data is the primary risk mitigation. If data is actually noise, prioritizing data legibility might distract governance from the real risk: algorithmic attention hijacking.
Context: Does measuring data transparency ensure safety if the underlying mechanism exploits finite human attention without transparency?
Source: Newsletter argues data rhetoric obscures ethical dimensions and colonization of cognitive space.
