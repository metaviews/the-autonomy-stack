## SIGNAL
**Title:** Internet Blackouts Trigger Offline Resilience
**Synthesized Content:** State-enforced digital suppression fails to dismantle dissent; instead, it catalyzes a shift to low-tech, high-bandwidth physical coordination like auditory signals and dense local networks, validating non-digital communication as a robust governance layer.
**Source Note:** Describes how authorities used social media blackouts to disrupt activism, forcing protesters to use alternative platforms, encryption, and offline methods like banging pots to sustain movement.

## PATTERN CANDIDATE
**Title:** Resource Wealth Undermines Domestic Political Legitimacy
**Synthesized Content:** Natural resource abundance attracts foreign investment and security partnerships that bolster state suppression capacity without strengthening the domestic social contract, creating a governance model where external rents outweigh internal accountability.
**Context:** Appears in resource-rich post-colonial states facing legitimacy crises.
**Forces:** External capital seeking extraction yields, foreign security contracts, local economic dependency.
**Consequences:** Erosion of state legitimacy, increased risk of violent suppression, and reliance on asymmetric foreign force to maintain authority.
**Cross-reference:** Also logged as STACK MATERIAL: Material Base - Resource Extraction Dynamics.

## STACK MATERIAL
**Title:** Digital Blackouts Compromise System Legibility
**Layer:** 3 (Legibility & Metrics)
**Synthesized Content:** Intentional information denial by the state creates a dual illegibility: it reduces state monitoring capability while blinding citizens to official data, forcing reliance on opaque rumor networks and fragmenting shared situational awareness.
**Framing:** Extends the principle that "power without legibility is unacceptable" by demonstrating how illegibility is weaponized by the state to evade contestation.
**Source Note:** Notes that government censorship tactics to disrupt communication inadvertently highlighted grassroots resilience and resilience of decentralized organization.
**Cross-reference:** Also logged as SIGNAL: Internet Blackouts Trigger Offline Resilience.

**Title:** Decentralized Leadership Challenges Hierarchical Authority
**Layer:** 4 (Decision Systems & Authority)
**Synthesized Content:** Movements adopting collective leadership structures bypass traditional representative intermediaries, creating resilient decision nodes that survive the detention or silencing of individual figures, reducing dependency on single points of failure.
**Framing:** Complicates the Stack's Decision Systems layer by demonstrating direct action efficiency over formalized representative structures during crises.
**Source Note:** Observes that the Mozambique movement thrives on collective leadership with no single figurehead, embodying self-management.
**Cross-reference:** Also logged as CONCEPT: Collective Leadership Architecture.

**Challenge:** Questions if the Stack's focus on domestic legibility and contestation is sufficient when authority is secured externally through resource rents and private contracts.
