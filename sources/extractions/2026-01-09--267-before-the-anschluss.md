## SIGNAL
**Sovereignty eroded through normalized constraint**
Sovereignty is lost not through invasion but through the psychological narrowing of what feels sensible to say. Power functions by framing resistance as irresponsible and stability as compassion, training populations to accept constraints as facts of life rather than political outcomes.
Source: Analysis of how modern social absorption replaces force with the internalization of limits.
Cross-reference note: See PATTERN CANDIDATE: Absorption relies on resignation not force.

## PATTERN CANDIDATE
**Absorption relies on resignation not force**
A dynamic where power succeeds by making opposition feel impractical rather than illegal. Coercion becomes optional once actors anticipate demands to avoid conflict, shifting the burden of restraint onto the weaker party. Stability is framed as relief; resistance is framed as cruelty because it risks reopening conflict.
Source: Historical case of Austrian surrender and contemporary Canadian political climate observations.
Cross-reference note: Also logged under SIGNAL: Sovereignty eroded through normalized constraint. See CONCEPT: Politics of Inevitability Defined as Strategy.

## STACK MATERIAL
**Layer 4: Legal forms repurposed to stage legitimacy**
Law is not destroyed first but hollowed out and repurposed as theatre. Authorities preserve the shell of procedure (votes, decrees) while removing substantive constraints, making the outcome feel settled and irreversible through familiar forms even as substance vanishes.
Source: Observation that the Austrian plebiscite staged will rather than discovered it.
Cross-reference note: See PATTERN CANDIDATE: Absorption relies on resignation not force.

## STACK MATERIAL
**Layer 5: Spectacle enforces unity beyond legal force**
Public performance of unanimity acts as enforcement, making dissent deviant by definition. Silence becomes suspicious because spectacle redefines refusal to participate as disruption rather than defense of sovereignty.
Source: Analysis of historical crowd and flag imagery described in the text.
Cross-reference note: See STACK MATERIAL: Layer 4: Legal forms repurposed to stage legitimacy.

## STACK MATERIAL
**Layer 6: Exhaustion weaponized to limit political imagination**
When populations face chronic strain, they prioritize order over the type of order offered. Resistance is abandoned not for safety but to prevent reopening conflict, shrinking the space of possibility and internalizing acceptance.
Source: Description of Austrian population exhaustion and elite beliefs in late 1930s.
Cross-reference note: See SIGNAL: Sovereignty eroded through normalized constraint.

## CONCEPT
**Politics of Inevitability Defined as Strategy**
A political strategy where power requires only resignation, framing sovereignty as unrealistic rather than illegal. It utilizes arguments of pragmatism, maturity, and interdependence to indistinguishably mask coercion, making constraints appear as unavoidable facts of life.
Source: Text definition of power dynamics in modern and historical contexts.
Cross-reference note: See PATTERN CANDIDATE: Absorption relies on resignation not force.

## CONCEPT
**Elite Pre-Compliance Defined in Context**
The practice where elites align with external narratives early to maintain relevance. They narrow policy options based on what will be tolerated elsewhere, policing public discourse via fear of being labeled extreme before any law enforces it.
Source: Note on Canadian elites aligning with external economic and political gravity.
Cross-reference note: See SIGNAL: Sovereignty eroded through normalized constraint.

## PROVOCATION
**Waiting for Obvious Resistance Complicates Contestability**
Assuming resistance will become justified only when events become clearly unacceptable is a vulnerability. If the threshold for acceptable shifts silently, waiting for recognition confirms the trap of inevitability before contestability can even occur.
Source: Critique of Austrian belief that a clearer moment would arrive later.
