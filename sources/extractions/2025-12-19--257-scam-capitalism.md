# 257: Scam Capitalism

**How Meta Uses Fraud as a Business Model**  
*2025-12-19*

---

### SIGNAL
**Fraud Emerges as Core Infrastructure Baseline**
Scam operations are no longer marginal exceptions but embedded revenue streams within core advertising infrastructure. Meta’s internal projections show up to 10% of revenue linked to prohibited ads, with enforcement calibrated to protect revenue flows rather than eliminate violations. Fraud is predicted, measurable, and tolerable within the platform logic. This represents a structural shift where deception is not a bug but a feature of the economic operating model.

*Source Note: Reuters investigation citing internal Meta documents regarding scam ad revenue and probability thresholds.*

### PATTERN CANDIDATE
**Enforcement Treated as Cost Rather than Boundary**
Enforcement is calculated as an operating cost rather than a hard prohibitory boundary. This dynamic appears in large-scale platforms facing regulatory scrutiny where the probability of fines is weighed against profit from violations. The consequence is authority becoming theater—a legal structure that exists but is functionally bypassed because compliance is less profitable than risk-taking. If ignored, external regulatory legitimacy erodes, and trust migrates to market alternatives or cynicism.

*Source Note: Analysis of political reaction and corporate response to fraud allegations.*
*Also logged as STACK MATERIAL: Decision Systems & Authority (Layer 4).*

### STACK MATERIAL
**Platform Advertising as Core Economic Infrastructure**
This insight maps to **Layer 1 (Material Base)** and **Layer 4 (Decision Systems)**. Advertising platforms are not passive intermediaries but active infrastructure deciding what appears credible and who absorbs harm. Meta’s role as the largest private infrastructure enabling scam distribution suggests material systems directly generate authority. This challenges the framing of digital platforms as marketplaces rather than governing bodies that allocate access and legitimacy.

*Source Note: Textual claim regarding platforms' role as governance systems and infrastructure providers.*

### CONCEPT
**Legitimacy Laundering via Digital Ad Placement**
A mechanism where paid placement confers implicit approval on third-party goods or actors. Fraud thrives on borrowing authority from the platform environment, which replaces traditional trust signals like newspapers or storefronts. This concept connects to Legibility & Metrics by showing how visual proximity to trusted brands acts as illegible social proof. It suggests trust is an economic asset that can be bought and monetized, bypassing authentic reputation systems.

*Source Note: Textual description of how scams utilize platform context for credibility.*

### PROVOCATION
**Technological Platforms Function as Governance Systems**
The text asserts advertising platforms are governance systems that decide credibility and safety, not neutral tools. This challenges the Stack's baseline assumption that governance is primarily the domain of law or explicit institutions. If private code and revenue models perform the same functions as public law without public contestation, the condition "authority must be contestable" is structurally impossible without external intervention, not just internal design.

*Source Note: Direct textual challenge to the "neutral intermediary" hypothesis.*

### OTHER
**Vulnerability Targeting as Algorithmic Default**
The observation that optimization systems treat vulnerability as a targeting signal (e.g., clicking interest leading to more aggressive sales) suggests care is absent from the material base of attention economics. This does not fit neatly into standard decision or material categories as it describes a physiological/social impact of technical parameters. Warrants inclusion in **Layer 5 (Knowledge & Intelligence)** as a design specification for systemic harm, or **Layer 6 (Human Capacity & Care)** as a failure of labor/protective norms.
