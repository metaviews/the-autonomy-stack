### CONCEPT
**Weaponized Bureaucracy**
A term for the strategic use of administrative processes (customs, audits, inspections) to create supply chain friction without declaring official conflict or tariffs. Unlike transparency-driven governance, this relies on opacity and slowness to destabilize adversaries or domestic populations while maintaining plausible deniability.
*Proposed Definition: The deliberate introduction of administrative cost and delay to achieve political or economic leverage.*
*Connects to: Legibility (Layer 3) — this is illegible power masked as procedure.*
*Source note: Describes Chinese export delays and US Customs audits as "grinding weaponization".*

**Affordability Threshold Principle**
A normative assertion that a nation's democratic stability is contingent on basic goods remaining within reach of labor value. "A country where a loaf of bread costs half a day's wages is not a democracy." This establishes a material floor for political legitimacy, suggesting legitimacy collapses when exchange value of labor falls below basic subsistence needs.
*Proposed Definition: The minimum ratio of labor time to essential goods consumption required to sustain democratic agency.*
*Connects to: Stack Principle — authority must be contestable (contestation fails under hunger/poverty).*
*Source note: Closing assertion in text regarding bread cost vs wages.*
