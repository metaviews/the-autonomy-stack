# Cornelius Castoriadis

## Core Relevance to Autonomy

Castoriadis is a central seed thinker for this corpus because his work treats autonomy as both individual and collective: not only personal self-direction, but the explicit capacity of a society to recognize and revise its own institutions.

For the Autonomy Stack, his relevance is strongest where agency, institution, imagination, and collective self-rule meet.

## Key Works and Source Records

- [Castoriadis source record](../../raw/castoriadis-source-record.md)
- *The Imaginary Institution of Society*
- *Figures of the Thinkable*

## Source Claims

- MIT Press describes *The Imaginary Institution of Society* as a major work that analyzes the social-historical world and emphasizes society as a creation of the society concerned, opening political theory toward autonomous and explicit self-institution.
- Stanford University Press describes *Figures of the Thinkable* as advancing the claim that human subjects and society are not predetermined, linking this to creative, autonomous activity.
- Papadimitropoulos's 2016 article frames Castoriadis's socialism through the project of individual and collective autonomy and relates it to collaborative commons.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Castoriadis helps name autonomy as an instituting capacity. Autonomy is not only the absence of external constraint; it is the capacity to participate in the creation, examination, and revision of the laws, meanings, and institutions that govern life.

This makes heteronomy more than domination by another actor. Heteronomy is also the condition in which institutions appear as given, natural, divine, technical, necessary, or otherwise beyond collective authorship.

The social imaginary matters because institutions do not rest only on rules. They rest on shared meanings that define what counts as real, possible, legitimate, and desirable.

## Stack Application

- **L2 Commons & Institutions:** Castoriadis clarifies why institutions are not neutral containers. They express and stabilize a society's self-understanding. Commons governance can be read as one form of explicit collective self-institution when participants recognize and revise the rules by which they govern shared resources.
- **L4 Decision Systems & Authority:** His autonomy/heteronomy distinction sharpens the Stack's concern with contestability. A decision system is heteronomous when its authority cannot be recognized as collectively authored or revised.
- **L5 Knowledge & Intelligence:** The social imaginary connects knowledge formation to institutional possibility. What a society can know, contest, or imagine is shaped by the meanings through which it interprets itself.

## Tensions and Cautions

- Castoriadis should not be treated as simply "supporting" the Stack. His work should be used to challenge whether the Stack's own categories are sufficiently self-revising.
- The relation between individual autonomy and collective autonomy can produce tension. Collective self-rule can enable individual agency, but it can also harden into institutions that claim collective authority against dissent.
- Later commons-oriented readings should be distinguished from Castoriadis's own texts.
