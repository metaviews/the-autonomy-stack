# Ivan Illich

## Core Relevance to Autonomy

Illich is central for autonomy-preserving tools. His work asks when institutions and technologies extend human capacity, and when they monopolize functions people previously held for themselves.

## Key Works and Source Records

- [Wave 1 source records](../../raw/wave-1-source-records.md)
- *Tools for Conviviality*

## Source Claims

- Open Library describes *Tools for Conviviality* as a 1973 book about technology, institutionalized expertise, technocratic elites, and practical knowledge.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Illich helps distinguish tools that expand agency from systems that convert users into dependents. This is directly relevant to agentic governance: an agentic tool can either preserve human judgment or create radical monopoly over action and knowledge.

## Stack Application

- **L3 Legibility & Metrics:** Technical systems can obscure dependency under the appearance of service.
- **L5 Knowledge & Intelligence:** Expertise can become monopolized by institutions or tools.
- **L6 Human Capacity & Care:** Autonomy requires maintaining practical capacity, not only receiving outputs.

## Tensions and Cautions

- Conviviality should not be reduced to usability or friendliness.
- Anti-institutional critique still needs a theory of maintenance and shared accountability.
