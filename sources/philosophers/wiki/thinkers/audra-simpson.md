# Audra Simpson

## Core Relevance to Autonomy

Simpson is central for refusal as a political practice and for autonomy under settler states that claim jurisdictional completion.

## Key Works and Source Records

- [Wave 2 source records](../../raw/wave-2-source-records.md)
- *Mohawk Interruptus*

## Source Claims

- Duke University Press describes *Mohawk Interruptus* as examining Kahnawa:ke Mohawk sovereignty, refusal of American or Canadian citizenship, and one sovereign political order nested within another.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Simpson helps the corpus treat refusal as governance, not absence of engagement.

## Stack Application

- **L2 Commons & Institutions:** Indigenous political orders may exceed settler institutional forms.
- **L4 Decision Systems & Authority:** Jurisdiction and legitimacy can be contested by refusal.
- **L3 Legibility & Metrics:** Research and recognition can misread political life through colonial categories.

## Tensions and Cautions

Agents should not generalize Indigenous refusal outside its situated political and national contexts.
