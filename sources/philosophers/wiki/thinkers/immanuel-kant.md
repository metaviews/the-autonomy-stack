# Immanuel Kant

## Core Relevance to Autonomy

Kant is essential because modern autonomy discourse runs through his account of self-legislation, reason, moral law, and dignity.

## Key Works and Source Records

- [Wave 1 source records](../../raw/wave-1-source-records.md)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entries on Kant and Kant's moral philosophy

## Source Claims

- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes autonomy as central to Kant's critical philosophy and moral philosophy.
- The SEP entry on Kant's moral philosophy explains autonomy through the rational will as author of the law that binds it.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Kant provides the strongest moral-philosophical account of autonomy as self-legislation. For the Stack, this is a necessary reference point, but not sufficient: Kantian moral autonomy does not by itself account for material, relational, institutional, or collective conditions of agency.

## Stack Application

- **L4 Decision Systems & Authority:** Legitimate authority can be tested against whether those bound by norms can recognize themselves as authors of them.
- **L5 Knowledge & Intelligence:** Reason and self-governance are linked, but the Stack must ask whose reasoning conditions are materially and socially supported.

## Tensions and Cautions

- Kant can overemphasize rational agency if read apart from dependency, care, disability, and material conditions.
- The Stack should distinguish moral autonomy from political, relational, and collective autonomy.
