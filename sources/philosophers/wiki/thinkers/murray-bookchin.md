# Murray Bookchin

## Core Relevance to Autonomy

Bookchin connects autonomy to ecology, hierarchy, and local democratic institution-building. His relevance is strongest where material systems and political forms produce or block collective self-rule.

## Key Works and Source Records

- [Wave 1 source records](../../raw/wave-1-source-records.md)
- *The Ecology of Freedom*

## Source Claims

- Open Library describes *The Ecology of Freedom* as a synthesis of ecology, anthropology, philosophy, and political theory tracing conflicting legacies of freedom and domination.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Bookchin helps frame domination as ecological and institutional, not merely interpersonal. Autonomy requires dismantling hierarchy and building democratic forms capable of governing material life.

## Stack Application

- **L1 Material Base:** Ecological conditions are autonomy conditions.
- **L2 Commons & Institutions:** Libertarian municipalism offers one model of local democratic institution.
- **L4 Decision Systems & Authority:** Hierarchy is a decision architecture, not only a social attitude.

## Tensions and Cautions

- Bookchin's critique of other anarchist tendencies should be handled in later source-specific pages.
- Municipalism may reproduce exclusion if participation and material access are unequal.
