# James C. Scott

## Core Relevance to Autonomy

Scott is central to autonomy because he shows how state legibility can destroy the local knowledge and social practices through which people govern their own lives.

## Key Works and Source Records

- [Wave 1 source records](../../raw/wave-1-source-records.md)
- *Seeing Like a State*

## Source Claims

- Yale University Press describes *Seeing Like a State* as a critique of top-down social planning and high-modernist schemes that attempt to engineer society and environment.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Scott clarifies legibility as ambivalent. Making a population visible to authority can support coordination, but it can also simplify, erase, and control local forms of agency.

## Stack Application

- **L3 Legibility & Metrics:** Scott is a core source for the danger of administrative simplification.
- **L4 Decision Systems & Authority:** State planning converts knowledge into enforceable authority.
- **L2 Commons & Institutions:** Local institutions carry knowledge that central systems often cannot see.

## Tensions and Cautions

- The Stack values legibility, but Scott warns that legibility can be a tool of domination.
- Local knowledge should not be romanticized; it still requires accountability.
