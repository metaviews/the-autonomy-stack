# Emma Goldman

## Core Relevance to Autonomy

Goldman brings autonomy into the domains of speech, sexuality, prison, patriotism, feminism, and everyday life. Her relevance is not only doctrinal anarchism, but insistence that freedom must be lived against social and state coercion.

## Key Works and Source Records

- [Wave 1 source records](../../raw/wave-1-source-records.md)
- *Anarchism and Other Essays*

## Source Claims

- The Project Gutenberg record lists *Anarchism and Other Essays* as Goldman's 1910 collection, with essays on anarchism, prisons, patriotism, women's emancipation, marriage, and love.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Goldman clarifies autonomy as embodied and socially dangerous. Autonomy is constrained not only by law, but by respectability, gender norms, nationalism, carceral power, and moral surveillance.

## Stack Application

- **L4 Decision Systems & Authority:** Goldman foregrounds state coercion, prison, and enforced morality as attacks on agency.
- **L6 Human Capacity & Care:** Autonomy includes affective, sexual, and relational freedom.
- **L3 Legibility & Metrics:** Respectability politics and public labeling shape whose autonomy is visible as legitimate.

## Tensions and Cautions

- Goldman should not be reduced to expressive individualism; her anarchism is social, relational, and anti-capitalist.
- Later readings should handle political violence and state repression with source-specific care.
