# Isaiah Berlin

## Core Relevance to Autonomy

Berlin is important because his negative/positive liberty distinction helps separate freedom from interference from freedom as self-mastery or self-determination.

## Key Works and Source Records

- [Wave 1 source records](../../raw/wave-1-source-records.md)
- *Two Concepts of Liberty*

## Source Claims

- Oxford Academic describes *Two Concepts of Liberty* as emphasizing negative liberty, positive liberty, value pluralism, and the danger of positive liberty being perverted into totalitarian claims.
- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy discusses Berlin's distinction between negative liberty as absence of obstacles or interference and positive liberty as control, self-mastery, or self-realization.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Berlin gives the corpus a warning: autonomy language can become coercive when authorities claim to know a person's "true" freedom better than the person does. This is directly relevant to care, expertise, and agentic systems.

## Stack Application

- **L4 Decision Systems & Authority:** Positive liberty can justify coercion if contestability is absent.
- **L3 Legibility & Metrics:** Authority can redefine freedom in ways that make coercion appear emancipatory.

## Tensions and Cautions

- Berlin should not be used to reject all positive accounts of autonomy.
- The Stack needs both non-domination and capacity-building, while guarding against coercive self-mastery claims.
