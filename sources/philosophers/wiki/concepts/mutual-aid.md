# Mutual Aid

## Working Definition

Mutual aid is reciprocal support organized by participants to meet needs, sustain capacity, and preserve agency without reducing people to clients, dependents, or administrative cases.

## Associated Thinkers

Kropotkin is the central seed thinker. Goldman and Graeber extend mutual aid into anarchist practice, direct action, and anti-bureaucratic organizing. Spade and Ward connect mutual aid to contemporary crisis survival and everyday anarchist institutions.

## Source Claims

- The Project Gutenberg record for Kropotkin's *Mutual Aid* describes the work as arguing that cooperation is a fundamental force in nature and human society.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Mutual aid reframes autonomy as socially produced. People are not autonomous because they need no one; they are autonomous when relations of support do not become relations of domination.

## Stack Application

Mutual aid is relevant to commons, care infrastructure, material base resilience, and distributed resistance.

## Tensions and Cautions

Mutual aid can be romanticized. Without material resources, accountability, and endurance, it can become emergency improvisation rather than durable infrastructure.
