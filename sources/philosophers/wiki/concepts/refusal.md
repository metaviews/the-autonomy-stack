# Refusal

## Working Definition

Refusal is the political act of declining recognition, classification, participation, or translation when those demands reproduce colonial or dominating authority.

## Associated Thinkers

Audra Simpson centers refusal in relation to sovereignty and settler recognition. Glen Coulthard and Leanne Betasamosake Simpson connect refusal to Indigenous resurgence, land, and grounded normativity.

## Source Claims

- Duke University Press presents *Mohawk Interruptus* as a study of sovereignty, recognition, and refusal.
- University of Minnesota Press presents Coulthard and Leanne Simpson's works as central to Indigenous resurgence and critiques of colonial recognition.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Autonomy sometimes requires withholding cooperation from systems that demand legibility as the price of existence.

## Stack Application

Refusal informs consent, data minimization, non-participation rights, protocol exit, and the design of agent-accessible corpora that do not force every community into extractive legibility.

## Tensions and Cautions

Refusal should not be misread as silence or lack of governance. It can be an active jurisdictional and ethical stance.
