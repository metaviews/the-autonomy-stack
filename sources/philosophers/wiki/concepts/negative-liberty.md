# Negative Liberty

## Working Definition

Negative liberty is freedom understood as absence of external interference, obstruction, or coercion within some domain of action.

## Associated Thinkers

Isaiah Berlin is the central seed thinker. Mill is a planned later-wave reference for liberal individuality and limits on interference.

## Source Claims

- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes negative liberty as absence of obstacles, barriers, or constraints.
- Oxford Academic describes Berlin's *Two Concepts of Liberty* as emphasizing negative liberty and value pluralism.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Negative liberty is crucial for protecting autonomy from interference, but it can miss forms of dependence, incapacity, internalized domination, or infrastructural control.

## Stack Application

Negative liberty is most useful for identifying coercive interference, overreach, and non-contestable authority.

## Tensions and Cautions

Freedom from interference does not guarantee the capacity to act.
