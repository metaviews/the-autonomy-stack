# State Legibility

## Working Definition

State legibility is the process by which complex social life is simplified into categories, records, metrics, and administrative forms that authorities can see, compare, manage, tax, police, or reform.

## Associated Thinkers

James C. Scott is the central seed thinker. Foucault helps connect legibility to discipline, knowledge, and subject formation. Audra Simpson sharpens the corpus question of when refusing legibility preserves autonomy.

## Source Claims

- Yale University Press describes Scott's *Seeing Like a State* as a critique of top-down social planning and state schemes to improve the human condition.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Legibility is double-edged. It can support coordination and accountability, but it can also erase local knowledge, flatten difference, and make people governable at the cost of autonomy.

## Stack Application

This concept directly informs Layer 3: who controls what can be seen and contested.

## Tensions and Cautions

The Stack needs legibility, but Scott warns that legibility can become domination when it serves the viewer more than the viewed.
