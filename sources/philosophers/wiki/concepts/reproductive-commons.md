# Reproductive Commons

## Working Definition

Reproductive commons are shared infrastructures of care, subsistence, social reproduction, and everyday survival that sustain autonomy outside market or carceral dependence.

## Associated Thinkers

Federici connects commons to reproductive labor, enclosure, and feminist autonomy. Spade shows how mutual aid can become survival infrastructure under crisis conditions.

## Source Claims

- PM Press presents Federici's work as linking capitalism, gendered labor, enclosure, and commons.
- Verso presents Spade's *Mutual Aid* as a guide to collective care and solidarity.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Autonomy depends on the infrastructures that reproduce life. A framework that ignores care and subsistence will treat agency as abstract and disembodied.

## Stack Application

This concept supports attention to maintenance, care work, community capacity, and non-extractive knowledge infrastructure.

## Tensions and Cautions

Care infrastructure can be exploited when responsibility is shifted onto unpaid communities without resources or political power.
