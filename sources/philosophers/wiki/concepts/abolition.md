# Abolition

## Working Definition

Abolition is the replacement of carceral and punitive systems with life-sustaining institutions, collective care, and material conditions that reduce harm without expanding domination.

## Associated Thinkers

Gilmore grounds abolition in the analysis of prisons, racism, capitalism, and organized abandonment. Spade connects abolition to mutual aid, crisis survival, and non-carceral support.

## Source Claims

- UC Press presents Gilmore's *Golden Gulag* as an account of prison expansion in California.
- Verso presents Spade's *Mutual Aid* as a practical handbook for solidarity and collective care.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Abolition treats autonomy as inseparable from the institutions that make survival possible. It rejects governance that responds to social failure by increasing capture, punishment, or abandonment.

## Stack Application

Abolition is relevant to agentic governance wherever enforcement, moderation, safety, or compliance systems risk becoming punitive substitutes for repair.

## Tensions and Cautions

Abolition should not be reduced to non-intervention. It requires positive infrastructure for care, accountability, conflict response, and material support.
