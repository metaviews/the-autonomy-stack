# Heteronomy

## Working Definition

Heteronomy is the condition of being governed by norms, institutions, categories, or authorities that appear external, given, natural, technical, divine, inevitable, or otherwise unavailable for collective authorship and revision.

## Associated Thinkers

Castoriadis, Kant, and Foucault illuminate different forms of heteronomy: social, moral, and subject-forming.

## Source Claims

- Castoriadis contrasts autonomy with heteronomy in relation to social self-institution.
- Kant contrasts autonomy with moral principles grounded outside the rational will.
- Foucault shows how subjects are formed through power and knowledge.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Heteronomy is not only domination by a visible ruler. It can appear as inherited institution, administrative category, technical necessity, expert closure, social norm, or internalized self-conception.

## Stack Application

The Stack treats heteronomy as a warning sign: agency is weakened when people cannot see, contest, or revise the systems that govern them.

## Tensions and Cautions

Not all external constraint is heteronomous in the same way. Shared rules can enable autonomy when they remain legible and contestable.
