# Moral Autonomy

## Working Definition

Moral autonomy is the capacity of a rational agent to be bound by principles understood as self-legislated rather than imposed by external authority, inclination, or command.

## Associated Thinkers

Kant is the central seed thinker. Berlin is relevant because positive liberty and self-mastery inherit some autonomy language while raising political dangers.

## Source Claims

- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes Kant's moral philosophy as grounded in the autonomous rational will.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Moral autonomy gives the corpus a rigorous account of self-legislation, but it must be supplemented by social, material, and relational accounts of how people become capable of self-governance.

## Stack Application

Moral autonomy helps clarify legitimacy: those bound by norms should be able to recognize themselves as authors rather than mere subjects.

## Tensions and Cautions

Moral autonomy can become abstract if detached from care, coercion, disability, poverty, social formation, or institutional exclusion.
