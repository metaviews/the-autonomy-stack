# Rootedness

## Working Definition

Rootedness is the grounding of autonomy in place, obligation, memory, relationship, and material conditions rather than abstract choice alone.

## Associated Thinkers

Simone Weil frames rootedness as a human need tied to obligation. Indigenous resurgence thinkers connect grounded autonomy to land, relation, and practices of self-determination.

## Source Claims

- Routledge presents Weil's *The Need for Roots* as a work on duties, needs, and social reconstruction.
- University of Minnesota Press presents Leanne Simpson and Coulthard's works as land-based and resurgence-oriented interventions.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Autonomy can be damaged by uprooting even when formal choice remains. Durable self-rule requires sustaining the relations and places that make agency meaningful.

## Stack Application

Rootedness informs local context, land and material base, community memory, and resistance to placeless optimization.

## Tensions and Cautions

Rootedness can be misused as exclusionary nostalgia. The autonomy question is whether attachment supports self-determination without hardening into domination.
