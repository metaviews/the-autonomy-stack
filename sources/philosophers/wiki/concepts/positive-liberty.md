# Positive Liberty

## Working Definition

Positive liberty is freedom understood as self-mastery, self-direction, self-realization, or the capacity to act according to one's own purposes.

## Associated Thinkers

Berlin is central for the distinction and the warning. Kant is relevant through autonomy as self-legislation.

## Source Claims

- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes positive liberty as control, self-mastery, self-determination, or self-realization.
- Oxford Academic describes Berlin as warning that positive liberty can be perverted into totalitarian claims.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Positive liberty names the capacity side of autonomy. It is indispensable because freedom requires more than non-interference, but dangerous when authorities claim the right to force people into their "true" freedom.

## Stack Application

Positive liberty maps to the Stack's concern with enabling conditions for agency across material, institutional, epistemic, and care layers.

## Tensions and Cautions

Capacity-building must remain contestable. Otherwise, liberation can become coercion.
