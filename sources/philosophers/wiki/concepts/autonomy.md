# Autonomy

## Working Definition

Autonomy is the capacity of persons and collectives to participate meaningfully in the formation, revision, and contestation of the norms, institutions, tools, and relations that shape their lives.

## Associated Thinkers

Castoriadis, Kant, Berlin, Arendt, Foucault, Kropotkin, Goldman, Illich, and Bookchin each illuminate different dimensions of autonomy.

## Source Claims

- Kant is a central source for moral autonomy as self-legislation.
- Castoriadis frames autonomy as individual and collective self-institution.
- Berlin distinguishes autonomy-related positive liberty from negative liberty, while warning about coercive uses of self-mastery language.

## Interpretive Synthesis

The corpus treats autonomy as plural. It is moral, political, relational, material, epistemic, and institutional. No single tradition fully contains it.

## Stack Application

Autonomy is the Stack's central concern: whether the conditions for agency are possible, sustained, and contestable across all six layers.

## Tensions and Cautions

Autonomy can be narrowed into individual choice, inflated into coercive collective self-mastery, or abstracted away from material dependence.
