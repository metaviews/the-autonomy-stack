# Commons Self-Governance

## Working Definition

Commons self-governance is the capacity of communities to steward shared resources through locally legitimate rules, monitoring, repair, and participation.

## Associated Thinkers

Ostrom provides empirical institutional analysis of common-pool resource governance. Kropotkin, Federici, and anarchist traditions connect commons to cooperation, subsistence, and autonomy.

## Source Claims

- Cambridge presents Ostrom's *Governing the Commons* as a study of institutions for governing common-pool resources.
- PM Press presents Federici's work as linking commons, reproduction, and feminist anti-capitalist struggle.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Commons are not unmanaged resources. They are governed relations that preserve autonomy when those affected by rules can shape, revise, and enforce them without external capture.

## Stack Application

This concept informs shared knowledge bases, local protocols, agent-accessible resources, and governance of common infrastructure.

## Tensions and Cautions

Commons can be enclosed by markets, states, platforms, or expert systems. They can also fail internally if participation, accountability, or repair are weak.
