# Subjectivation

## Working Definition

Subjectivation is the process by which people become recognizable to themselves and others as certain kinds of subjects through practices, norms, institutions, classifications, and knowledge systems.

## Associated Thinkers

Foucault is the central seed thinker. Scott helps connect subject formation to administrative legibility.

## Source Claims

- The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes Foucault's account of power as productive and distributed through practices, institutions, and forms of knowledge.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Subjectivation complicates autonomy because agents are not formed outside power and then constrained by it. They are partly made through the categories and practices that govern them.

## Stack Application

Subjectivation matters whenever governance systems classify, score, train, normalize, or personalize subjects.

## Tensions and Cautions

Subject formation does not eliminate agency. It changes the question: how can formed subjects still contest the conditions of their formation?
