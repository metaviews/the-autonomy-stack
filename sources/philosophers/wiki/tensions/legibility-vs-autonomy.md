# Legibility vs. Autonomy

## Tension

Governance requires some legibility, but the act of making people and practices legible can simplify, expose, control, or erase the local knowledge through which autonomy is exercised.

## Thinkers Who Illuminate It

James C. Scott, Michel Foucault.

## Source Claims

Yale University Press describes Scott's *Seeing Like a State* as a critique of top-down social planning and state schemes to improve society.

## Interpretive Synthesis

Legibility is not inherently emancipatory or oppressive. Its political meaning depends on who sees, who is seen, what gets simplified, and whether the seen can contest the categories.

## Stack Application

This tension sits at the center of Layer 3 and agentic governance. Agents need structured data, but structured data can govern the people it describes.

## Open Questions

What forms of agent-readable structure preserve contestability rather than enabling capture?
