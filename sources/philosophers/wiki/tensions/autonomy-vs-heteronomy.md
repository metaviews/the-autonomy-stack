# Autonomy vs. Heteronomy

## Tension

Autonomy requires that people and collectives can recognize, contest, and revise the norms that govern them. Heteronomy appears when those norms are treated as external, natural, technical, divine, inevitable, or otherwise beyond authorship.

## Thinkers Who Illuminate It

Castoriadis, Kant, Foucault.

## Source Claims

The seed source records support Castoriadis as a thinker of explicit self-institution and Kant as a thinker of moral self-legislation.

## Interpretive Synthesis

The same system can enable autonomy in one register and heteronomy in another. Shared law can be self-authored; it can also harden into untouchable authority.

## Stack Application

The Stack's contestability requirement is a practical response to heteronomy.

## Open Questions

How can institutions remain stable enough to coordinate while revisable enough to avoid heteronomy?
