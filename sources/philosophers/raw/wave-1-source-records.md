# Wave 1 Source Records

This file preserves seed source records for the first wave of the philosopher autonomy corpus. It records starting points for later source ingestion; it is not a substitute for reading the works.

## Peter Kropotkin

- **Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution** — Project Gutenberg record for Kropotkin's 1902 collection. The record describes the work as challenging competition-centered social Darwinism and arguing for cooperation and mutual aid in nature and human societies.  
  Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/4341

## Emma Goldman

- **Anarchism and Other Essays** — Project Gutenberg record for Goldman's 1910 collection, including essays on anarchism, prisons, patriotism, women's emancipation, marriage, and love.  
  Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2162

## Gustav Landauer

- **Revolution and Other Writings: A Political Reader** — PM Press record for the edited English collection of Landauer's political writings, including *Revolution* and essays across his political biography.  
  Source: https://pmpress.org/index.php?l=product_detail&p=161

## David Graeber

- **Debt: The First 5,000 Years** — official David Graeber site record for the 2011 Melville House book. The record frames the book as a long history of debt, credit systems, debtors and creditors, and political/religious disputes around obligation.  
  Source: https://davidgraeber.org/books/debt-the-first-5000-years/

## James C. Scott

- **Seeing Like a State** — Yale University Press record for Scott's critique of top-down social planning, state simplification, and high-modernist schemes.  
  Source: https://yalebooks.yale.edu/9780300252989/seeing-like-a-state/

## Murray Bookchin

- **The Ecology of Freedom** — Open Library record for Bookchin's work on hierarchy, social ecology, freedom, domination, and ecological crisis.  
  Source: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL1897570M/The_Ecology_of_Freedom

## Ivan Illich

- **Tools for Conviviality** — Open Library record for Illich's 1973 book on technology, tools, institutionalized expertise, radical monopoly, and practical knowledge.  
  Source: https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2848898W/Tools_for_Conviviality

## Hannah Arendt

- **Hannah Arendt** — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry, especially the sections on action, freedom, natality, and plurality.  
  Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/arendt/

## Michel Foucault

- **Michel Foucault** — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry, including discussion of power as productive and distributed through practices, institutions, and knowledge.  
  Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/foucault/

## Immanuel Kant

- **Immanuel Kant** — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry describing autonomy as central to Kant's critical philosophy and moral philosophy.  
  Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant/

- **Kant's Moral Philosophy** — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry, especially the discussion of autonomy and the autonomous will.  
  Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-moral/

## Isaiah Berlin

- **Two Concepts of Liberty** — Oxford Academic chapter record in *Liberty*, with abstract emphasizing negative liberty, positive liberty, value pluralism, and the danger of positive liberty being perverted into totalitarian claims.  
  Source: https://academic.oup.com/book/7968/chapter/153281672

- **Positive and Negative Liberty** — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry discussing Berlin's negative/positive liberty distinction and its relation to autonomy.  
  Source: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/Win2018/entries/liberty-positive-negative/
