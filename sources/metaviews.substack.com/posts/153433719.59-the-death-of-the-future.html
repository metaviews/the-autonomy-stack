In a world once dominated by promises of "progress," "growth," and "development," authority has long anchored its legitimacy in visions of the future. Political leaders, corporations, and institutions drew their power by offering a better tomorrow—a future brimming with prosperity, technological marvels, and solutions to today’s problems. But what happens when the collective faith in that future collapses?

Today, we’re witnessing the unraveling of this grand narrative. The death of the future isn’t just about pessimism or existential dread; it’s another example of authority in crisis.

The Myth of Progress Has Broken

The 20th century was defined by its belief in linear progress: every step forward brought us closer to a utopian ideal. Industrialization, the space race, and globalization were heralded as milestones on humanity’s inevitable ascent. But the 21st century has laid bare the emptiness of this myth.

Climate collapse, staggering inequality, and the rise of authoritarian politics have made it clear: the promises of the past were naïve and deliberately misleading. Instead of prosperity, we face resource scarcity and austerity. Instead of freedom, we’ve got digital surveillance and algorithmic suppression. Instead of unity, we have discord and disparity.

This failure to deliver on promises of a better future has disillusioned entire generations. Millennials and Gen Z—drowning in student debt, facing precarious jobs, and inheriting an uninhabitable planet—are particularly skeptical of anyone claiming to hold the keys to progress.

Authority has always depended on our collective imagination. Governments promise security and prosperity. Religions offer salvation or enlightenment. Corporations sell visions of convenience and happiness. These promises are future-oriented—they ask us to endure hardship today for rewards tomorrow.

When we no longer believe those rewards will come, the foundation of authority erodes. Why trust a government that promises growth when wages stagnate? Why obey institutions that urge us to sacrifice for a future that looks increasingly bleak? When the future is dead, so too is the trust that sustains authority.

The Denial of Contemporary Politics

Amid this collapse of collective belief, contemporary politics finds itself in a paradoxical state of denial. On one hand, leaders continue to invoke the rhetoric of a brighter tomorrow, doubling down on promises of growth, innovation, and renewal. On the other hand, the public, and often the leaders themselves, seem painfully aware that these promises lack substance.

This dissonance fuels a sense of cognitive disillusionment. Politicians campaign on visions of the future, but those visions rarely inspire genuine hope. Instead, they feel like hollow gestures—the last gasps of a system clinging to outdated narratives. The gap between the rhetoric and reality deepens mistrust, further eroding the authority they seek to maintain.

What Replaces the Future?

The collapse of collective faith in the future doesn’t leave a vacuum; it opens the door to new modes of authority and imagination:

  1. Nostalgia: When the future is unthinkable, people turn to the past. This is fertile ground for reactionary politics, which often romanticize a mythical "better" time. From MAGA to monarchist revivals, nostalgia wields authority by rejecting progress altogether.

  1. Doomsday Cultures: The rise of apocalyptic thinking, whether in religious extremism or secular preppers, offers authority by embracing the inevitability of catastrophe. In these frameworks, leaders claim legitimacy by preparing followers for survival, not salvation.

  1. Local Resilience Movements: On the other side of the spectrum, grassroots movements and mutual aid networks derive authority from immediate, tangible action. These groups don’t promise a grand future but focus on meeting present needs and building adaptive systems. Their power lies in pragmatism, not prophecy.

  1. Techno-Authoritarianism: Even as faith in progress collapses, tech companies have stepped in to offer a bleak new vision of the future. From AI-managed societies to data-driven governance, this form of authority derives legitimacy from its promise to optimize survival, while managing the dystopia.

The Role of Collective Imagination

If authority is to survive the death of the future (so that it might be reborn), it will need to root itself in new forms of collective imagination. The challenge is to envision futures that feel credible, inclusive, and worth striving for.

This may mean abandoning the old frameworks of linear growth and endless development. Instead, we could prioritize regenerative practices, communal well-being, and systems that acknowledge ecological limits. Futures that are shared, rather than dictated, might offer the most resilient forms of authority in a fragmented world.

The death of the future is not the end of hope. It’s an opportunity to reimagine what we want from our lives and our leaders. Authority no longer rests on promises of progress. It must earn legitimacy by addressing the crises of the present while opening space for new, pluralistic visions of tomorrow.

What futures do we dare to imagine? And how can we build systems that make those futures possible? These questions will define the next phase of authority.
